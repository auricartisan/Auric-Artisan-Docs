# Limits and accuracy

## The articles

The articles are educational explanations of colour science, perception and practice. They are not standards documents; where an article describes a standard (such as WCAG or a CIE formula), the standard itself is the authority.

Articles on vision and light — for example colour vision deficiency or light and the body clock — are written to educate, not to diagnose or advise on health. Consult a professional for medical questions.

## The demos

Demos are teaching models computed in your browser. They follow the published formulas their articles describe, but:

- colours on screen depend on your display, its settings and the light around you;
- simulations of colour vision deficiency show typical cases, not any particular person's sight;
- numbers are rounded for display.

Use the site's tools for measurement work (see [Related](related.md)).

## Reading times and categories

Reading times are the authors' estimates. Three small categories (Computational, Measurement, Workflows) are folded into Colorimetry and Digital on the Learn page.

## Progress tracking

"Read" means scrolled past 55% of the page in this browser. It does not know whether you actually read the text, and it is lost if the browser's site data is cleared.
