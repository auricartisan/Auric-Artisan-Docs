==============================================================================
READ WHAT A TRANSFORM MOVED
==============================================================================
Interpret the clip overlay, the colour difference cards and the hue circle on
the Lab tab.
Area: Website › Tools › Image and file tools › LUT Lab   Updated: 2026-09-25

Everything below the before and after pair on the Lab tab describes the
difference between the two images, for the transform currently shown: a look
at its strength, or a loaded file.

THE PAIR
========

  - Before is the scene, with its pixel count beside the label, for example
    57600 for a 320 × 180 scene.
  - After is the scene through the transform, with the clipped share beside
    the label, for example 0.83% clipped.

The clip overlay
----------------

With What the transform crushed switched on, the After image is hatched with
diagonal stripes where the transform clipped a pixel:

  Stripes | Meaning
  --------+---------------------------------------------------------------
  Pink    | A channel was driven to white (255) that was not at 255 before
  Blue    | A channel was driven to black (0) that was not at 0 before

Pixels that were already at 0 or 255 and stayed there are not marked. Switch
the overlay off to see the clean result; the figures do not change.

WHAT IT MOVED
=============

  Card:    mean ΔE₀₀
  Meaning: The average CIEDE2000 colour difference between each pixel before
           and after

  Card:    95th percentile
  Meaning: The difference that 95% of pixels are at or below; shows how big
           the typical large change is

  Card:    largest
  Meaning: The single largest difference on any pixel

  Card:    clipped
  Meaning: The share of pixels with at least one channel driven to 0 or 255 by
           the transform; the card turns red above 1%

  Card:    the old test said
  Meaning: The share of output pixels with any channel at 0 or 255, whatever
           it was before

The last card is the test an earlier version of the tool used. It counts every
saturated or black or white pixel in the result, even those the transform did
not touch, so it is shown only for comparison. On the Colour patches or
Saturation field scene, compare it with clipped to see the difference.

WHERE IT MOVED THINGS TO
========================

The hue circle tests the transform on 72 colours arranged round a ring in
CIELAB, at a constant lightness (L* 60) and chroma (C* 40), one every 5° of
hue.

  - The angle round the circle is the CIELAB hue; the distance from the centre
    is the chroma. Faint rings mark every 20 units of chroma.
  - Each hollow dot is a test colour before the transform. The filled dot,
    joined to it by a thin line, is where the transform moved it.
  - A pink ring around a filled dot marks a test colour the transform clipped.

Not every colour at L* 60, C* 40 fits inside sRGB. Test colours that do not
fit are pulled in towards the centre, keeping their hue, until they do. The
caption says how many: 13 of 72, whatever the transform, because the ring is
the same every time. This is done before the transform runs, so the shift you
see belongs to the transform, not to the gamut.

The circle cards
----------------

  Card:    largest hue shift
  Meaning: The largest turn of hue on any test colour, in degrees, with its
           direction (+ or −)

  Card:    mean hue shift
  Meaning: The average size of the hue turn across all 72, in degrees

  Card:    largest L shift*
  Meaning: The largest change in CIELAB lightness, with its direction

  Card:    clipped on the circle
  Meaning: How many of the 72 test colours the transform clipped

Hue alone rarely tells the whole story: most looks move lightness and chroma
at the same time, often by more. The CSV export of the hue sweep gives all
three for every test colour; see Write a .cube file [write-a-cube-file.txt].

READING THE NUMBERS TOGETHER
============================

  - A look with a small mean ΔE₀₀ but a large largest changes most colours a
    little and a few a lot. Find the few with the overlay or the hue circle.
  - A high clipped figure on your own picture means detail is being lost in
    highlights or shadows. Lower Strength, or for the tone-map, change
    Tone-map exposure.
  - A consistent twist on the hue circle, all dots turning the same way, is a
    hue rotation. Dots moving inwards or outwards are chroma changes.

THE METHOD TAB
==============

The Method tab explains the method in five sections, chosen with the buttons
under The chain:

  Section:       Sample order
  What it shows: How a `.cube` indexes its rows, with the first eight rows of
                 a 2 × 2 × 2 table

  Section:       Interpolation
  What it shows: How trilinear interpolation works, and the worst error on an
                 identity at sizes 2 to 65

  Section:       Clipping
  What it shows: Seven test pixels judged by the old test and by the clip test
                 used now

  Section:       Round trip
  What it shows: Writing a table and reading it back, and whether an identity
                 survives at various sizes

  Section:       Linear light
  What it shows: The tone-map applied in linear light compared with applying
                 it to encoded values
