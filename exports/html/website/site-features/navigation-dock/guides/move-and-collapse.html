Move and collapse the dock

Drag the navigation dock, flip it to the other side, and understand how it collapses on desktop and on phones.

Move the dock up or down

  1. Point at the dock so it opens.
  2. Press and hold the grip (six dots) at its top.
  3. Drag up or down. The dock can sit anywhere between 10% and 90% of the window's height.
  4. Let go.

Result: the dock stays at that height on every page, in this browser.

Move the dock to the other side

Either:

  • drag the grip across the middle of the window, then let go; or
  • select Flip dock side (the arrow at the bottom of the dock).

Result: the dock moves to the other edge and stays there on every page. The outline panel opens on the side facing the page.

How the dock collapses

On screens wider than 700 pixels:

  • after a page loads, the dock stays open for about 1.6 seconds so you can see it, then collapses;
  • collapsed, it is a thin gold line, 64 pixels tall, flush with the edge. It is faint until you point at it;
  • it opens when you point at it, click it or tab into it, and collapses about half a second after you move away;
  • it stays open while the page outline is open, while you are dragging it, and while a button in it has keyboard focus.

On a phone

On screens 700 pixels wide or narrower:

  • the dock is a horizontal bar centred at the bottom of the screen. If its buttons do not fit, swipe it sideways;
  • the grip and Flip dock side are not shown, and the dock cannot be dragged;
  • it starts collapsed. Open it from the page utilities bar with Navigate (on the narrowest phones this is the only way). On slightly wider screens, a round button with a list icon (Show navigation) also opens it;
  • it closes again when you tap anywhere outside it, and a dock opened with its round button closes by itself after about five seconds.

When the dock is hidden

  • While the screen saver or an eye rest is showing.
  • While the mobile drawer is open.
  • Inside the panes of split view, which have their own toolbar.
  • On the sign-in and verification pages.
