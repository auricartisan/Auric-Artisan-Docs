Cones, opponent channels and the cone table

How the Visual Pathway turns a screen colour into cone signals and opponent channels, how to read the Pipeline tab and the cone figure, and how to install your own cone table.

The idea in brief

Your retina has three kinds of cone. The L (long-wavelength) cones respond most to yellowish-red light, the M (medium) cones to green light and the S (short) cones to violet-blue light. Their sensitivities overlap heavily: in the published CIE 2006 fundamentals the L and M cones peak at 566.8 nm and 541.2 nm, only about 25 nm apart, and the S cones at 441.8 nm. A colour you see is summarised by three numbers, the cone signals, one per type.

Early in the visual pathway these three signals are recombined into opponent channels: one that compares L with M (red against green), one that compares S with the sum of L and M (blue against yellow), and one that adds L and M (roughly, lightness). A neutral grey drives neither chromatic channel, which is why you never see a "reddish green" or a "bluish yellow".

How the tool gets from a hex colour to opponent values

The Pipeline tab draws the chain as a row of steps:

StepWhat happens
sRGBThe 8-bit, gamma-encoded screen value
linearDecoded through the sRGB transfer curve (IEC 61966-2-1)
XYZConverted to CIE XYZ with the sRGB primaries at D65
LMSConverted to cone signals with the Hunt-Pointer-Estévez matrix
cone contrastMarked not held: the tool has no adapting white to express cone contrast against
opponentL − M, S − (L+M)/2 and L + M
stagesThe retina-to-V4 stages read these values

The Hunt-Pointer-Estévez matrix is published for XYZ and is scaled so that the D65 white point gives equal L, M and S signals. That is what puts every neutral grey at zero on both chromatic channels. The Neutral off-axis readout on the Lab tab checks it: the largest departure of a nine-step grey ramp from zero is about 2.2 × 10⁻⁴, which is the rounding of the published four-decimal matrix.

These axes are not DKL

The opponent space of Derrington, Krauskopf and Lennie (DKL), used widely in vision science, is built on cone contrast relative to a stated adapting white, with its chromatic axes scaled so they carry no luminance change. The tool's axes are plain differences of absolute cone signals, and its luminance channel gives L and M equal weight, where their published contribution to luminance is closer to two to one. They are useful axes and they are the tool's own. Nothing on the page is reported in cone-contrast units.

Use the Pipeline tab

The rail has two groups of buttons.

Show

  • The chain — the step row above and a table of seven colours (#000000, #404040, #808080, #C0C0C0, #FFFFFF, #3A8CFF, #E03131) with their L − M and S − (L+M)/2 values to four decimals. Greys are highlighted when they sit at zero.
  • The opponent axes — the same table, with a note giving the worst neutral departure.
  • The sensitivity curve — a table of pupil sizes (2, 3, 5 and 8 mm) against the peak of the tool's sensitivity curve (always 4.0 c/deg) and the published photopic peak range (2–6 c/deg).

Against

  • What it was (default) — adds columns showing the values an earlier version of this page produced, marked as shipped, so you can see how large each correction was.
  • Hide — shows the current values only.

Try it: with The chain and What it was selected, compare the white row. The current columns read 0.0000; the "as shipped" columns do not.

Read the cone figure

On the Lab tab, The front end: where the two cone curves actually sit plots three curves from 390 to 730 nm: L in red, M in green and S in blue, each scaled to peak at 1. Dashed vertical lines mark the published peaks, labelled 442, 541 and 567 nm. The note under the figure explains what you are seeing.

The built-in curves are the tool's own. They are smooth and plausibly shaped, but both the L and M curves peak at 590 nm, on top of each other, where the published curves peak 25 nm apart. That is why L − M peak gap reads 0 nm and is highlighted. These curves draw the figure and nothing else: every cone signal the stages use comes from the matrix described above, not from this table.

Install your own cone table

You can replace the figure's curves with a published table, for example the CIE 170-1:2006 2° cone fundamentals, which the page does not include.

  1. Prepare a CSV or JSON file with four columns in this order: wavelength in nm, L, M, S.
    • CSV: one row per line, values separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or spaces. Lines that begin with # or a letter (such as a header row) are skipped.
    • JSON: an array of four-number rows, or an object with a rows array.
  2. Open the Data tab.
  3. Drop the file on Drop a CSV or JSON under Install a cone table, or select that area to choose the file.
  4. Read the message at the top of the page: Cone table installed — L peaks at N nm, or Not installed: followed by the reason.

The table is refused when it has fewer than eight rows, when a row has fewer than four columns or a non-numeric value, when the wavelengths do not increase, or when its L and M curves peak less than 5 nm apart.

Once installed, the cone figure, the curve labels, L − M peak gap and the note under the figure change to the new table, and the settings file and CSV record the file name as the cone table with reportable set to true. The stages do not change, because they never used the table. The installed table lasts until you reload the page.

Simulate a colour vision deficiency

Colour vision on the Lab rail applies a dichromacy simulation to the stimulus before the stages: Protanopia (no L cones), Deuteranopia (no M cones) or Tritanopia (no S cones). The tool uses the three projection matrices of Viénot, Brettel and Mollon (1999), applied in cone space as they are defined, at full strength. Try Protanopia with the default blue grating, then with a red base colour, and watch the LGN, L − M map. For a fuller set of simulations over your own images, use Perception and Vision.

Take the numbers away

The Export tab runs any list of hex colours through the same front end and gives L, M, S and the three opponent values; see Export and share. Put a grey in the list: its two chromatic values should read zero.
