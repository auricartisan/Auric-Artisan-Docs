Use a measured reflectance

Load a spectrophotometer reflectance file in place of the synthesised presets, and go back to the presets.

The twenty presets are invented curves. If you have a measured reflectance spectrum, from a spectrophotometer or a published dataset, you can render it instead.

Prepare the file

  • A plain-text file: .csv, .tsv or .txt.
  • Two columns per line: wavelength in nm and reflectance. Columns may be separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or spaces. Lines that are not two numbers are skipped.
  • Reflectance from 0 to 1. A file in percent is detected automatically: if any value is above 1, every value is divided by 100. Values outside 0–1 after that are clipped.
  • At least eight rows, covering at least 420 to 680 nm.

Load it

  1. Drop the file on the spectrum plot on the Lab tab, or on Drop a reflectance file under Measure the material on the Data tab. You can also click either area (or press Enter or Space on the Data tab's area) to choose the file.
  2. Read the message: for example 41 rows accepted, read as percent — the register now says verbatim, or Not installed: with a reason.

What changes

  • The file is resampled onto the 5 nm grid from 380 to 780 nm by straight-line interpolation between your rows; beyond the ends of your file, the first and last values are held flat.
  • The material menu now holds a single entry, named after your file with — measured, and it is selected. The twenty presets are hidden.
  • Reflectance on the Lab tab reads measured — and your file name, and the plot legend names your file.
  • On the Data tab, The twenty pigment reflectances entry becomes Verbatim, with your file name, the number of rows and the wavelength span.
  • Exports mark the reflectance as MEASURED, from your file.
  • Go back to the presets appears on the Data tab.

Every other control, including dielectric or conductor, roughness, lobe and geometry, applies as before.

Go back

Select Go back to the presets on the Data tab. The message Back to the twenty synthesised presets confirms it. The loaded curve is also lost when you reload the page; links do not carry it.

You should now see your own material rendered under any light and lobe.
