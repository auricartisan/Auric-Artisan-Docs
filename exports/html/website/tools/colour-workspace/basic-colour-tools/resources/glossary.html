Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the colour, contrast, print and workspace terms used in the Basic Color Tools and Colour Tools documentation.

Terms are in alphabetical order.

TermMeaning
AchromatopsiaComplete absence of colour vision; everything appears in shades of grey.
ACEScgA linear, wide-gamut RGB space used in film and visual-effects rendering.
Adobe RGB (1998)A wider-gamut RGB space used in photography and print workflows, especially for greens and cyans.
APCAAccessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm, a contrast method proposed for WCAG 3 that accounts for which colour is text and which is background.
APCA LcAPCA's lightness-contrast value, from about −108 to +106; its size, together with font size and weight, predicts readability.
AnalogousA harmony of hues next to each other on the colour wheel, typically about 30° apart.
Black pointThe darkest colour a printing process can actually produce, which is never a pure black.
CCTCorrelated colour temperature, in kelvin; an estimate of how warm or cool a light-like colour appears.
ChromaHow colourful a colour is compared with a grey of the same lightness.
ChromaticityThe colour quality of a light apart from its brightness, usually given as CIE x and y.
CIE 1931 chromaticity diagramA horseshoe-shaped chart of all colours people can see, used to compare gamuts.
CIELAB (Lab)A CIE colour space with lightness (L) and two opponent axes (a green–red, b blue–yellow), designed for measuring colour difference.
CIELuvA CIE perceptual space often used for lights and displays.
CMYKThe four process inks of printing: cyan, magenta, yellow and black (key).
Colour harmonyA set of hues chosen by their positions on the colour wheel, such as complementary or triadic.
Colour spaceA system of numbers for describing colours, such as sRGB, Lab or OKLCH.
Colour stopA colour fixed at a position along a gradient.
ComplementaryTwo hues opposite each other on the colour wheel.
Contrast ratioThe WCAG 2.x measure of luminance difference between two colours, from 1:1 to 21:1.
CVDColour-vision deficiency, often called colour blindness.
D50, D65Standard white points: D65 is average daylight (used by sRGB and screens); D50 is used in print and for CSS lab() and lch().
DeuteranopiaA red–green colour-vision deficiency caused by missing green-sensitive cones; the most common type.
Display P3A wide-gamut RGB space used by many modern phones and monitors.
DockWhere a minimised panel is kept, out of view, until you restore it.
Dot gainThe darkening of mid-tones in print as ink spreads into the paper.
EasingHow the pace of a blend changes along a gradient, such as ease in or ease out.
ΔE (delta E)A number for how different two colours look. ΔE 76 is the simple Lab distance; ΔE2000 (CIEDE2000) is the more accurate modern formula.
Focused panelThe panel you selected last; it sits in front and receives keyboard shortcuts and Save to Library.
GamutThe range of colours a colour space, screen or printer can reproduce.
Gamut clippingReplacing a colour that is outside a gamut with the nearest colour inside it.
HEX codeA six-digit (or three-digit) hexadecimal way of writing an sRGB colour, such as #D3AF37.
HSL, HSV, HWBCylindrical versions of RGB: hue with saturation and lightness, saturation and value, or whiteness and blackness.
HueThe attribute that makes a colour red, yellow, green, blue and so on, given as an angle from 0° to 360°.
InterpolationCalculating the colours between two stops; the colour space used changes how the middle looks.
JzAzBzA perceptual colour space designed to work for high dynamic range as well as normal images.
k-meansA clustering method used to find the main colours of an image by grouping similar pixels.
Level (Basic, Advanced, Both)The Catalogue filter that shows simpler tools, advanced tools, or all of them.
Linear RGBRGB with the gamma curve removed, so the numbers are proportional to light energy.
Luminance (relative)How bright a colour is on a 0 (black) to 1 (white) scale, used by WCAG contrast.
MetamerismWhen two colours match under one light and differ under another.
MonochromaticA set of colours with one hue and different lightness.
Named colour systemA catalogue of colours with names or codes, such as Pantone, RAL, NCS or the CSS named colours.
OKLab, OKLCHA modern perceptual colour space (and its lightness, chroma, hue form) where equal steps look roughly equal; supported in CSS.
PanelA movable, resizable window inside the page that holds one tool.
ProtanopiaA red–green colour-vision deficiency caused by missing red-sensitive cones; reds also look darker.
Rec.2020A very wide-gamut RGB space defined for ultra-high-definition and HDR video.
Role paletteA palette where each colour has a job, such as canvas, ink, primary or focus, chosen so pairs meet a contrast target.
SaturationHow intense a colour is relative to its brightness, in HSL or HSV terms.
Soft-proofingPreviewing on screen how a design is likely to look when printed.
sRGBThe standard RGB colour space of the web and most screens.
StageThe surface in the Workspace view where panels open.
TACTotal area coverage: the maximum combined percentage of CMYK inks a press allows.
Tetradic, squareFour hues spaced around the colour wheel; in these tools both use four hues 90° apart.
TriadicThree hues 120° apart on the colour wheel.
TritanopiaA rare blue–yellow colour-vision deficiency caused by missing blue-sensitive cones.
WCAGWeb Content Accessibility Guidelines. Their contrast levels: AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and interface parts; AAA needs 7:1 for normal text.
XYZ, xyYCIE tristimulus values that other colour spaces are built on, and the chromaticity-plus-luminance form of them.
YCbCr, YUV, YIQLuma and colour-difference encodings used in video and broadcast.
