Color Psychology

Enter one to six colours and read their combined warmth and energy, emotion words, personality traits, brand-fit notes and cultural meanings by region.

Color Psychology gives a quick reading of what colours tend to suggest. For up to six colours it shows a combined palette mood (how warm and how energetic the set feels, with emotion words and personality traits), and for each colour its emotional associations, personality, the kinds of brand it tends to suit, and what it can mean in different cultures and traditions.

The readings are organised by hue family. The tool decides each colour's family (for example Red, Teal or Gray) from its hue, saturation and lightness, looks up that family's associations, and adjusts them for very light or very dark colours.

Color Psychology is in the launcher's Explore group.

Open it

Open the launcher and select Color Psychology. See Open the Colour Tools. It starts with one colour: the colour you opened with, or gold (#D3AF37).

Screen tour

  1. A toolbar with + Color, − Color, Random and a count such as 2 colors.
  2. Colors (1–6): one colour field per colour, labelled Color 1, Color 2 and so on.
  3. Palette mood:
    • a strip of your colours;
    • a Warmth meter from Cool to Warm and an Energy meter from Calm to Stimulating;
    • a sentence such as The palette feels warm and inviting, with high energy.;
    • up to eight emotion tags and up to twelve trait tags drawn from all the colours.
  4. One reading per colour:
    • a large swatch, the heading Color 1 — Red, and an estimated colour temperature with a label;
    • Emotion, Personality and Brand fit;
    • Cultural meanings: a table of regions or traditions, each with a positive association (+) and, where the data has one, a negative one (−).

Hue families

The tool uses 14 families plus a fallback: Red, Orange, Yellow, Lime, Green, Teal, Cyan, Blue, Purple, Magenta, Pink, Black, White, Gray, and Other for anything that does not fit.

Very light colours (HSL lightness above 78 %) gain the emotions Soft and Calming, the traits gentle and airy, and lower energy. Very dark colours (lightness below 25 %) gain Serious and Grounded, the traits intense and deep, lower energy and a little less warmth.

How the mood is worked out

Each family has a warmth score and an energy score from −100 to +100. The palette mood averages them across your colours:

AverageSentence
Warmth above 30warm and inviting
Warmth below −30cool and considered
Warmth in betweenbalanced
Energy above 30high energy
Energy below −10low energy
Energy in betweenmoderate energy

Cultural meanings

The cultural table covers regions and traditions such as Western, China, Japan, Korea, India, Hindu, Buddhism, Tibetan, Thailand, Egypt, Middle East, Islam, Africa, South Africa, Ireland and the Netherlands. Which ones appear depends on the colour family; the Western row appears for almost every family.

Tasks

Check the mood of a brand palette

  1. Enter your main brand colour in Color 1.
  2. Select + Color to add each supporting colour (up to six).
  3. Read Palette mood: the two meters, the sentence and the tags.

Result: a short description of how the palette is likely to come across, useful for a brief or a design review.

Check a colour for an international audience

  1. Enter the colour.
  2. Read Cultural meanings under its reading, and look for negative associations in the markets you care about.

Result: a list of associations to discuss with people who know those markets.

Explore random combinations

  1. Select Random. Every colour is replaced with a new random colour and the readings update.

Result: quick inspiration for moods you might not have considered.

Outputs and exports

The tool has no export. Copy colours from their fields, and quote the readings as you need.

Accuracy and limits

  • Colour associations are cultural generalisations drawn from common design and marketing references. They vary between people, generations, industries and contexts, and they change over time.
  • The same family reading applies to every colour in the family; two different blues get the same associations unless one is very light or very dark.
  • Cultural meanings are brief summaries, not research. Check important decisions with people from the audience in question.
  • The colour temperature figure is an estimate for light sources and can read Indeterminate for some colours.
