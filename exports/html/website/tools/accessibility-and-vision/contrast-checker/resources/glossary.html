Contrast Checker glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used by the Contrast Checker.

AA and AAA — WCAG conformance levels. AA is what most laws and contracts require; AAA is stricter and not required everywhere.

APCA — The Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm, a newer contrast model being developed for a future version of WCAG. The Contrast Checker does not calculate it.

Contrast ratio — The WCAG measure of how different two colours are in lightness, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white).

Hex colour — A colour written as a # and six hexadecimal digits for red, green and blue, such as #1A1400, or three digits as shorthand, such as #F00.

Large text — Text of at least 18.66 pixels in bold or 24 pixels otherwise. It needs less contrast than body text.

Relative luminance — How much light a colour gives off, from 0 for black to 1 for white, weighted towards green as in human vision.

sRGB — The standard colour space of the web, which the ratio formula assumes.

UI & graphics — Interface components, focus indicators and meaningful graphics, which need 3:1 against the colour next to them.

WCAG — The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines published by the W3C. The checker uses the WCAG 2.1 contrast formula and thresholds.
