Check contrast and colour vision

Check every step of a ramp as a background for a text colour with WCAG 2.2 and APCA, and view the ramp as seen with three colour-vision deficiencies.

Check text on every step

  1. Build your ramp on the Ramp tab.
  2. Open the Contrast tab.
  3. Under Text colour, select the swatch and choose the colour your text will be. The default is white, #FFFFFF.
  4. Read Your ramp, checked both ways. The note beside the heading names the text colour, for example against #FFFFFF.

Every step is treated as a background for text in the chosen colour, and measured by two methods at once.

WCAG 2.2

The WCAG 2.2 contrast ratio compares the relative luminance of the two colours, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white).

RatingRatioMeaning
AAA7:1 or moreBody text at the enhanced level
AA4.5:1 or moreBody text at the minimum level

failBelow 3:1Not enough for text

APCA

APCA, the Advanced Perceptual Contrast Algorithm (version APCA-W3 0.1.9 here), measures lightness contrast in units called Lc. It treats dark text on a light background and light text on a dark background differently, so it is signed:

  • a positive Lc means dark text on a lighter background;
  • a negative Lc means light text on a darker background.

With white text, a dark step gives a negative figure, for example −99.8 for #0B3D5C.

Absolute LcGood for
90 or moreany text
75 or morebody text
60 or morelarge text
45 or moreheadlines only
30 or morenon-text only
Below 30not for text

Black text on white gives Lc 106.04; white text on black gives −107.88.

Why two figures

WCAG 2.2 and APCA disagree by design: they measure different things on different scales. A step can pass one and fail the other. Both are always shown so you can see the disagreement rather than being handed whichever one passes.

The samples

Under the table, three boxes show the sentence "The quick brown fox" in your text colour on the first, middle and last steps, so you can judge by eye.

What the evidence table shows

At the top of the Contrast tab, The contrast figure passed text its own algorithm fails compares, for five fixed pairs, what an earlier version of this tool printed (It printed) with what APCA gives (APCA gives) and the difference (Out by). The earlier version used the wrong scale and was missing two parts of the algorithm, so it reported contrast about 1.4 times too high: 161.80 for black on white, where APCA gives 106.04. The footnote under your own table says how far the old figure is off on your ramp, and how many steps it would wrongly have passed at Lc 60.

WCAG 2.2 was already right confirms the WCAG figures: black on white gives exactly 21.00, #767676 on white gives 4.54, and #595959 gives 7.00.

View the ramp with a colour-vision deficiency

On the Ramp tab, under Show it as seen by, choose:

ButtonSimulates
Normal visionNo simulation
ProtanProtanopia: no working long-wavelength (red) cones
DeutanDeuteranopia: no working medium-wavelength (green) cones
TritanTritanopia: no working short-wavelength (blue) cones

The simulation redraws the ramp, the dots on the hue circle, the three ramps on the Spaces tab and the samples on the Contrast tab. It uses the model of Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at full severity, applied in linear light.

The figures, the contrast table, the copied values and the exports always use the true colours.

What to look for

  • Two neighbouring steps that become hard to tell apart under a simulation are a problem for a data scale.
  • A ramp that runs from red to green can collapse to a single-hue ramp under Protan or Deutan; one that changes lightness steadily stays readable.

What the simulation does not cover

It simulates complete loss of one cone type (dichromacy). It has no setting for milder, partial forms (anomalous trichromacy), which are more common, so the case the tool cannot show is the more likely one.
