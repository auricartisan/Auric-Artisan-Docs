Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the legal, privacy, billing and security terms used in Auric Artisan's policies.

These definitions explain terms as the policies use them. They are not legal definitions and do not change the policies.

Acceptable use — The rules on what you may and may not do with the platform; set out in the Acceptable Use Policy.

Anonymise — To remove the link between data and a person, so the data remains but no longer identifies anyone. Auric Artisan anonymises payment records on account erasure.

APCA — The Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm, a newer way to measure text contrast, used alongside WCAG ratios in the Analyzer.

Authorised testing — Analysing a site, page or asset you own, manage or have explicit permission to test. The only kind of web analysis the policies allow.

CCPA / CPRA — California's consumer privacy laws, named in the Privacy Policy among the laws it aims to follow where they apply.

Chargeback — A payment reversed by your bank or card issuer.

Consent (cookie consent) — Your choice, recorded in your browser, about which optional cookie categories may run.

CSRF — Cross-site request forgery: a trick that makes your browser send a request you did not intend. The site uses a CSRF cookie and checks to prevent it.

Data minimisation — Collecting and keeping only the data that is needed.

Do Not Track (DNT) / Global Privacy Control (GPC) — Browser signals asking sites not to track you. Auric Artisan's analytics honours both.

DPDP Act — India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, named in the Terms and Privacy Policy.

Effective date — The date a policy took effect. Last updated is the date of its latest change.

Entitlement — What a purchase gives your account, such as a plan's benefits or a credit balance. A refund ends the entitlement.

Erasure (right to) — The right to ask for personal data to be deleted, where applicable.

First-party — Set or run by auricartisan.com itself, rather than by another company.

GDPR / UK GDPR — The European Union's and the United Kingdom's data-protection laws.

GST — India's Goods and Services Tax. Auric Artisan is not currently registered for GST, so no GST is charged and receipts are not GST invoices.

HSTS — HTTP Strict Transport Security: a rule telling browsers to use only secure HTTPS connections to the site.

Input tax credit — A business's ability to offset GST paid on purchases; not available on Auric Artisan's receipts.

Licence (noun) — Permission to use something under conditions. The License Agreement gives you a limited licence to use the platform.

Mandate — A standing permission for a merchant to charge you again. Auric Artisan stores none.

Overage — API use beyond the included monthly allowance, paid from prepaid credits only if you turn it on.

PCI-DSS — The card industry's security standard. Razorpay, which handles card details, is PCI-DSS compliant.

Penetration test — An authorised simulated attack to find weaknesses. The Security page states that no external one has been done.

Plan pass — A one-time purchase of a paid plan for a fixed term; it does not renew.

Portability (right to) — The right to receive a copy of your data in a usable format.

Pro rata — In proportion; for example, refunding the unused part of a partly used balance.

Processing licence — The limited permission you give Auric Artisan to process what you submit, only as needed to run the tool.

Responsible disclosure — Reporting a security weakness privately and giving time to fix it before telling anyone else.

Retention — How long data is kept.

SLA — Service-level agreement: a contractual promise of uptime. Auric Artisan has none yet.

SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / HIPAA — Security and privacy certifications or regimes. The Security page states Auric Artisan holds none of them.

Telemetry — Usage data a program sends back to its maker. The browser extension and VS Code extensions send none.

Third party — A company other than Auric Artisan, such as Cloudflare, Razorpay, Google Fonts or Resend.

TLS — Transport Layer Security, the encryption behind HTTPS.

WCAG — The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines published by the W3C. Level AA and AAA are its common targets.
