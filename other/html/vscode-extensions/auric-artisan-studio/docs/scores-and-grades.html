Scores and grades

How the overall, contrast, accessibility and code-health scores and their letter grades are calculated, with a worked example.

Every score in the Studio is a number from 0 to 100, where 100 means nothing was found. The formulas are fixed, so two projects with the same score are comparable on what was scanned. Scores are a way to prioritise work and track progress. They are not an accessibility certification: static checks cover only part of WCAG, and manual testing is still needed.

The letter grade

GradeScore
A+95 or more
A90 to 94
B80 to 89
C70 to 79
D55 to 69
Fbelow 55

The same scale is used for the overall score, the code-health score and the brand audit.

The overall score

The overall score is the one shown in the status bar, the Overview ring and the Studio's Home.

  • When the project has both resolvable contrast pairs and markup or CSS files, the overall score is the average of the contrast and accessibility sub-scores, rounded.
  • When only one of them applies, the overall score is that one.
  • When neither applies, there is no score and the grade shows —.

Code health is scored separately and does not change the overall score.

The contrast sub-score

Every contrast pair the scanner resolved earns credit, measured against its own target (3:1 for large text and 4.5:1 otherwise, or 4.5:1 and 7:1 at the AAA threshold):

The pair…Credit
meets AAA for its text size1.00
meets the selected target0.95
is below the target but at least 3:10.55
is below 3:1ratio ÷ 3 × 0.35

A pair graded against an assumed page background (the rule sets only color: and the background was taken from body, html or :root) counts half, because it is an inference rather than a reading. Every other pair counts fully.

The contrast sub-score is the weighted average credit × 100, minus a colour-vision penalty: the share of pairs that pass for typical vision but weaken under colour-vision deficiency, × 25, capped at 10 points. Pairs classified as intentional branding are left out entirely. If there are no resolvable pairs, the contrast sub-score is omitted.

The accessibility (WCAG) sub-score

Each WCAG finding adds a penalty:

FactorValues
Impactcritical 6, serious 4, moderate 2, minor 0.7
Confidencehigh × 1, medium × 0.65, low × 0.35
Automationautomated × 1, assisted × 0.55, manual × 0
Best-practice hint× 0.5

The penalties are added up and divided by the square root of the number of markup and CSS files scanned, so a large project is not punished merely for its size. The sub-score is 100 minus that result, never below 0. A project with no findings scores 100. If no markup or CSS files were scanned, the sub-score is omitted.

Every finding counts, including repeats of the same success criterion. Suppressed findings are removed before scoring. Branding marks never remove WCAG findings.

The code-health score

Each code-health finding adds a penalty by category, scaled by confidence:

CategoryWeight
Possible secrets14
Security risks12
Merge markers10
Risky APIs4
Debug leftovers2
Oversized0.5
Task markers0.25
ConfidenceMultiplier
high1
medium0.75
low0.4

The total is divided by the number of files scanned, multiplied by 16, and subtracted from 100 (never below 0). The code-health score uses the same letter grades.

A worked example

A project has 10 contrast pairs and 5 markup files.

  1. Contrast: 6 pairs meet AAA (6 × 1.00), 2 meet AA (2 × 0.95), 1 is 3.2:1 (0.55) and 1 is 2.1:1 (2.1 ÷ 3 × 0.35 = 0.245). The average credit is 8.695 ÷ 10, so 86.95. One pair of the ten is a colour-vision risk: 0.1 × 25 = 2.5 points off. The contrast sub-score is 84.
  2. Accessibility: one serious, high-confidence, automated finding (4), two minor findings (0.7 each) and one best-practice hint (0.7 × 0.35 × 0.55 × 0.5, about 0.07). The total is about 5.47. Divided by √5 (about 2.24) that is about 2.45, so the sub-score is 98.
  3. Overall: (84 + 98) ÷ 2 = 91, grade A.

Colours used for scores

  • Status bar: the score item uses the theme's warning background below 90 and its error background below 70.
  • Studio: scores of 90 and above use the theme's pass colour, 70 to 89 the warning colour, and below 70 the fail colour. Every score is also written as a number and grade, so colour is never the only signal.

Priority in hovers

Issue hovers show Priority N/100 with a segmented meter and one of four pieces of advice: fix now (78 or more), fix soon (55 to 77), worth fixing (30 to 54) or low urgency (below 30). Priority is not part of any score. It starts from the finding's impact (critical 94, serious 78, moderate 56, minor 32), is scaled by confidence, and for contrast findings by how far the pair falls short of its target. Branding findings always show a low priority because they are outside the score.
