Guide and page outline

The page guide opened with ?, its guided tours, and the section rail on long company, legal and trust pages.

The guide is a help panel for the page you are on. Press ? (or select the question-mark Guide button in the header) and it opens by saying what the page is and what it is for. It offers a guided tour of the page, which highlights each important area in turn with a short explanation; a map of the tour's stops; quick links to other parts of the site; and recommended paths for common jobs, such as running an audit or finding a tool. The guide remembers how far you got in each page's tour, so you can pick up where you left off.

Long company, pricing, legal and trust pages also have a section rail: a row of section links pinned under the header, labelled "On this page", which highlights the section you are reading and jumps to any other.

A third outline, the Page outline button in the navigation dock, lists any page's headings; it is described in Navigation dock › Page outline.

Who it is for

  • First-time visitors who want to know what a page does.
  • Anyone who prefers a short walkthrough to exploring alone.
  • Readers of long policy and company pages who want to jump between sections.

Where to find it

  • Press ? on any page (when you are not typing in a field).
  • Select Guide (the question mark) in the header.
  • In the mobile drawer, tap Guide (Tours and shortcuts) under Your work.
  • In Spotlight, type > and choose Open the page guide.

The section rail appears by itself on the pages that have one.

Quick start

  1. Open https://auricartisan.com/tool/analyzer/ and press ?.
  2. The guide opens with Analyzer guide and a summary of the page.
  3. Select Take the guided tour.
  4. The first area of the page is highlighted with an explanation. Select Next, or press the Right arrow, to move on.
  5. Press Esc to leave the tour at any point.
  6. Press ? again later: the tour card now offers Pick up where you left off.

What you can do

  • Learn what any page is for, in one sentence.
  • Take, pause, resume and replay a guided tour of the page.
  • Jump straight to any tour stop.
  • Filter the guide by typing, or by tab: All, Tour, Tools, Pages, Learn, Help.
  • Follow quick links and recommended paths.
  • Jump between sections of long pages with the section rail.
