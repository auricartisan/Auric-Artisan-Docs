Content and Intellectual Property Policy

A plain-language summary of who owns what on Auric Artisan — your inputs and outputs, the limited licence you grant, the platform's own IP, open-source parts and exports.

Official page: https://auricartisan.com/legal/content-and-intellectual-property/ — version 2.2, effective 27 April 2026, last updated 23 May 2026. The official policy is authoritative.

Key points

  • You own what you provide and the outputs generated from it.
  • Auric Artisan receives only a limited licence to process your inputs and deliver results.
  • Palettes, themes, reports, exports and visual assets you generate are generally yours to use, subject to third-party rights.
  • Account, export, portfolio and billing features do not transfer ownership of your content.

Section by section

  1. User content and inputs — Prompts, files, URLs, screenshots, palettes, colour values, notes, drafts and code snippets remain yours. You must have the right to submit and transform them.
  2. Limited processing licence — By submitting content you grant a limited, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence, only as needed to process it, cache it where technically required, show results to you, and use aggregated or anonymised technical patterns to improve quality. Ownership does not transfer.
  3. Generated outputs — Colour-science outputs, theme tokens and CSS variables, web analysis reports, learning material you create, and simulation results are generally yours to use.
  4. Platform intellectual property — The code, design system, interface, branding, logos, documentation, workflows, analysis logic and model implementations belong to Auric Artisan or its licensors. No copying, reverse engineering, scraping or derivative works without written permission, except where law or an open-source licence allows.
  5. Open-source components — Covered by their own licences (MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD and others), which apply only to those components.
  6. Exports and your responsibility — You are responsible for how you use exports (PDFs, reports, images, code, CSS, tokens, palettes, data files, simulations, themes); check third-party rights before public or commercial use.
  7. Content removal and enforcement — Content that breaks the policy or the law may be removed or refused; takedown notices and legal requests may be acted on, sometimes without notice.
  8. Third-party content — Websites, fonts, images and libraries you analyse keep their own owners and licences; reports do not transfer rights in them.
  9. Changes — The policy may be updated.

In practice

  • You can use generated palettes and themes in commercial work. The FAQ page says the same: Auric Artisan "does not claim any rights over tool outputs".
  • The articles are Auric Artisan's work: brief quotation with attribution and a link is fine; full reproduction needs permission.
  • Analysing someone else's site does not give you rights in its content.
