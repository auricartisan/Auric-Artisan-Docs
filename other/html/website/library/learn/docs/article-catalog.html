Learn article catalogue

Every published Learn article, grouped by topic, with its number in the course, reading time, link and a one-line summary.

This catalogue lists all 113 articles in the Learn library, grouped by the seven categories the Learn page uses as filters. Within each group, articles are in course order: the number before each title is its place in the course, which is meant to be read from 1 to 113. Together they take about 64 hours to read.

Each entry gives the article number, the title (linked to the article), the reading time stated on the article, and a one-line summary taken from the article's own description. Titles keep the site's published spelling.

Foundations

What colour is, how it has been understood, and the ideas everything else builds on.

  • 1. What Color Is and How Humans See It — 45 min. An interactive, comprehensive guide to color: the physics of light, the biology of vision, photoreceptor science, trichromatic and opponent theories.
  • 2. The History of Color Science from Newton to Hering — 40 min. A deep, interactive history of color science: Aristotle and Alhazen, Newton's prism, Goethe's protest, Young's trichromatic theory.
  • 29. Color Order Systems: Munsell, NCS, Pantone, and RAL — 35 min. A deep, interactive guide to color order and notation systems: Munsell hue/value/chroma, the Natural Color System (NCS), Pantone spot colors, and RAL.
  • 38. Color Naming and Categorical Perception — 30 min. Where does green become blue? Languages carve the spectrum differently, and the words we have subtly shape what we see — explained interactively.
  • 45. Additive and Subtractive Color Mixing — 32 min. Why lights add to white and inks subtract to black: the two great models of color mixing, plus partitive optical mixing — explained interactively.
  • 49. The Vocabulary of Color: Hue, Saturation, Value, Tint, Tone, and Shade — 30 min. Hue, saturation, chroma, lightness, value, tint, tone, shade — the precise meaning of every color word, explained interactively.
  • 53. Warm and Cool Colors: The Temperature of Color — 29 min. Why oranges feel warm and blues feel cool, why warm colors come forward, and how warmth is always relative — the temperature of color, explained interactively.
  • 56. The Color Solid: Visualizing All Colors in Three Dimensions — 31 min. Color needs three numbers, so all colors form a solid. Spin the RGB cube, the HSL cylinder, the Lab solid, and see why real gamuts taper — interactively.
  • 65. Black, White, and Gray: The Achromatic Colors — 29 min. Perceptual lightness versus linear, the myth of middle gray, why black and white are relative, tinted neutrals, and using neutrals as the backbone of design.
  • 74. Primary Colors and Color Wheels: Why the Models Disagree — 30 min. Why there's no single 'true' set of primaries, how RYB, RGB, and perceptual wheels differ, and why complements and mixing disagree — interactively.
  • 84. Simultaneous Contrast: Why a Color Depends on Its Neighbors — 29 min. An interactive guide to simultaneous contrast: why an identical color looks different against different surrounds.
  • 86. Optical Mixing and Pointillism: The Third Kind of Color Mixing — 29 min. An interactive guide to optical (partitive) color mixing: how the eye blends tiny adjacent colors at a distance.
  • 89. Grayscale: The Many Ways to Remove Color — 28 min. There is no single 'grayscale' — there are many, and they disagree. Luminosity, average, lightness, channels, and gamma, explored interactively.
  • 97. Color and Sound: Synesthesia, Color Organs, and the One Octave of Light — 28 min. Color organs, disagreeing pitch-to-color schemes, and the surprising fact that all of color is a single octave of light — explored interactively.
  • 108. The Stories of Famous Pigments: Color's Most Precious, Poisonous, and Peculiar — 29 min. An interactive history of famous pigments: ochre and the first cave paintings, ultramarine pricier than gold, Tyrian purple from sea snails.

Physics

Light itself: wavelength, spectra, emission, scattering and how surfaces and materials produce colour.

  • 3. The Physics of Light, Wavelength, and Spectrum — 50 min. A deep, interactive guide to the physics of light: the electromagnetic spectrum, wavelength and frequency, photon energy, blackbody radiation.
  • 4. Spectral Power Distributions and Why RGB Is Not Enough — 50 min. A deep, interactive guide to spectral power distributions: how light is actually shaped at each wavelength, the standard illuminants (D65, D50, A, F2.
  • 28. Color Temperature and White Balance — 35 min. A deep, interactive guide to color temperature and white balance: the Kelvin scale and blackbody radiation.
  • 33. How Colorants Work — 35 min. A deep, interactive guide to how colorants work: selective absorption, pigments vs dyes, why blue and yellow paint make green.
  • 40. Color in Nature — 30 min. The physics behind the blue sky, red sunsets, rainbows, and white clouds — scattering, refraction, and atmospheric color, explained interactively.
  • 48. Fluorescence, Phosphorescence, and Optical Brighteners — 33 min. Why a highlighter glows, why your white shirt is secretly blue, and why glow-in-the-dark stars fade slowly — luminescence in color, explained interactively.
  • 50. Polarization and Color — 34 min. A deep, interactive guide to how polarization makes and reveals color: linear polarization and Malus's law, photoelastic stress colors and birefringence.
  • 54. Thin-Film Interference and Iridescence — 32 min. Why a soap bubble blooms with color and shifts as you tilt it — the wave interference behind iridescence and structural color, explained interactively.

  • 80. Color at Depth: Why the Underwater World Is Blue — 30 min. An interactive guide to color underwater: how water absorbs sunlight wavelength by wavelength, why red vanishes first and the deep sea turns blue.
  • 93. The Color of Metals: Why Gold Is Gold and Silver Is a Mirror — 28 min. Why gold is yellow and copper red while silver is neutral — an interband absorption edge in the reflectance spectrum. Explored interactively.
  • 99. The Color of Gemstones: Why Ruby Is Red and Sapphire Is Blue — 29 min. Trace impurities that absorb light, and why the same chromium ion makes ruby red but emerald green — the color of gemstones, explored interactively.
  • 103. Patina, Rust, and Tarnish: How Color Changes as Metal Ages — 28 min. An interactive guide to how metal changes color as it ages: why copper turns green, iron turns orange, and silver turns black.
  • 110. The Colors of Animals: Camouflage, Warning, and Display — 30 min. Why animals are colored the way they are — camouflage, warning colors, mimicry, and angle-shifting structural color, explored with interactive demos.
  • 112. The Color of Heat: Why Hot Things Glow - Incandescence, Blackbody Radiation, and the Color of Stars — 30 min. How Planck's law and Wien's displacement law explain why heated things glow red then white then blue — and why the Sun is white. Explored interactively.

  • 57. Color Vision Across the Lifespan: Development, Aging, and the Yellowing Lens — 30 min. Why a newborn sees little color, why the lens yellows with age, and why older eyes lose blues and need more light — color vision over a lifetime, interactively.
  • 64. Tetrachromacy, Anomalous Trichromacy, and the Range of Human Color Vision — 31 min. A deep, interactive guide to variation in human color vision: anomalous trichromacy, potential tetrachromacy.

  • 78. The Evolution of Color Vision — 31 min. The dichromat bottleneck, the primate regain of a third cone, the fruit-finding advantage, and why color blindness is X-linked — interactively.
  • 85. The Perception of Transparency: How the Eye Sees Through Things — 30 min. An interactive guide to perceptual transparency: how the visual system decides a region is see-through, Metelli's transparency rules and scission.
  • 91. Chromatic Acuity: Why You See Detail in Brightness, Not Color — 29 min. The eye resolves fine detail in brightness but blurs color — which is why video and JPEG throw away most color data invisibly. Explored interactively.
  • 96. Color in Motion: Flicker Fusion, Spinning Disks, and Benham's Colors — 28 min. Spinning disks that fuse colors, Newton's disk to gray, and Benham's illusory colors — the color science of time, explored interactively.
  • 105. Contrast Sensitivity: The Spatial Frequencies You Can and Can't See — 29 min. An interactive guide to the contrast sensitivity function.
  • 111. Light and the Body Clock: Blue Light, Melanopsin, and Circadian Color — 30 min. How the color of light sets your body clock — melanopsin, melatonin, color temperature, and night modes, explored with interactive demos.

  • 92. Dominant Wavelength and Purity: Naming a Color by Its Spectrum — 28 min. An interactive guide to dominant wavelength and excitation purity: how to describe any color as a spectral hue plus a purity.
