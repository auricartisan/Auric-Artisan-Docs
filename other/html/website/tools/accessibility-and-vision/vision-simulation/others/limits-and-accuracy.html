Limits and accuracy

How the Vision Lab's simulations are made, how far to trust them, and what the lab is not.

What the simulations are

  • Educational visualisations. The page states they are "not a medical simulation, a diagnostic tool, or a substitute for professional eye care". They show how a typical case of a condition might affect what you see, to help design and teaching.
  • Per-colour conditions use published colour-vision models, calculated in linear light (colours are converted from screen values to values proportional to light before any maths):
    • Brettel (1997) projects colours onto the plane a dichromat can see, in LMS cone space.
    • Viénot (1999) is a simplified, single-plane version of the same idea.
    • Machado (2009) models reduced cone sensitivity, which suits the milder "-anomaly" forms and partial severities. The floating panels label their version "Machado-like". The models agree on the broad picture and differ in detail. Try more than one.
  • Field conditions are illustrative effects (blur, dimming, lost regions, glare, distortion) drawn over the image. They show the kind of loss, not a measured map of any real patient's field.
  • Severity is a dial on the model, not a milder diagnosis. The severity score (1 to 10) is an estimated visual-impact score that orders the collection; it is not a clinical grade.

What changes the result you see

  • Your screen. Screen colours are not calibrated. Brightness, colour profile, night modes and panel type all change what you see.
  • The room. Ambient light changes how colours look.
  • Your own vision. If you have a colour-vision deficiency yourself, a simulation of another deficiency is filtered through your own.
  • Real people vary. Two people with the same diagnosis can see differently.

Measurements

  • ΔE76 and ΔE2000 are calculated between the original and simulated colours. ΔE2000 follows perception more closely.
  • Collapse in the Workbench and Compare means a reference pair that was at least 12 apart (ΔE2000) falls within 8. Palette audit uses your chosen threshold, 5 by default.
  • Button label / fill and CTA figures are WCAG contrast ratios, calculated on the simulated colours of the mock-up.
  • Palette audit covers the 17 per-colour conditions only; field conditions are not colour transforms.

Images

  • Images are processed at a working size set by Quality (1024, 1600 or 4096 pixels), not always at full resolution.
  • Very large images take longer at the higher quality settings.

What the lab is not

  • Not a vision test or a diagnosis.
  • Not a guarantee that a design is accessible. Pair it with contrast checks and with testing by people who have these conditions.
  • Not a substitute for professional eye care.
