Vision Lab glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used by the Vision Lab.

Achromatopsia — Seeing no colour at all, only light and dark.

CAM (colour appearance model) — A model of how a colour looks in context: under a given light, surround and brightness. The panels offer CAT16, CAM16-UCS, CIECAM02 and Hunt-Stevens options.

CIE xy — A two-number description of a colour's hue and saturation (chromaticity), plotted on the CIE 1931 diagram.

CIEDE2000 (ΔE2000) — A formula for colour difference that follows human perception closely. Around 1 is barely noticeable.

Clinical default — The severity at which a condition is shown by default.

Collapse — Two colours that were clearly different becoming indistinguishable under a condition. The Workbench counts a reference pair as collapsed when it was 12 or more apart and falls within 8 (CIEDE2000).

Cone — A light-sensitive cell in the eye. L, M and S cones respond to long (red), medium (green) and short (blue) wavelengths.

Correlated colour temperature (CCT) — The colour of a white light, in kelvin: lower is warmer, higher is cooler.

CVD (colour-vision deficiency) — Reduced ability to tell some colours apart, often called colour blindness.

Deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia — Colour-vision deficiencies in which the green-, red- or blue-sensitive cones are absent.

Deuteranomaly, protanomaly, tritanomaly — Milder forms, in which those cones are present but weakened.

ΔE (delta E) — A measure of colour difference. ΔE76 is a simple distance in Lab colour space; ΔE2000 is the more perceptual CIEDE2000.

Field effect — A condition that changes how or where you see (blur, lost regions, glare, distortion) rather than changing each colour.

Illuminant — A standard light source used in colour calculations, such as D65 (average daylight).

Linear light — Colour values converted from screen encoding to values proportional to light, which the models need to work correctly.

LMS — The three cone responses (long, medium, short). Colour-vision models work by adjusting colours in LMS space.

Observer (2° or 10°) — The standard field of view assumed by colour maths: 2° for small or close things, 10° for large areas.

Per-colour transform — A condition in which each colour becomes another colour in a predictable way.

Prevalence — Roughly how common a condition is.

Severity — On the slider, how strongly the model is applied (0–100%). As a score, an estimated visual-impact rating from 1 to 10.

Specimen — What the Workbench stage shows: the Interface mock-up, the Palette strip or your Image.

White point — The colour treated as white under a given light.
