The specimen panel

How the family panel docks, resizes and closes, and how to use the Specimen tab's editor, variable axes, named instances, OpenType switches and presets.

Selecting a card opens that family in a panel. It is where you test a family and find everything about it.

Where the panel sits

  • On a wide screen (wider than about 1,180 pixels), the panel docks to the right edge as a column of the page. The page moves aside, nothing is covered, and you can keep selecting cards: the panel simply shows the next family.
    • Drag the grip on the panel's left edge to make it wider or narrower (380 to 720 pixels; 460 at first). Double-click the grip to go back to 460.
    • With the grip focused, ← and → resize it by 16 pixels, or 48 with Shift.
    • The width you choose is remembered.
  • On a narrower screen, the panel is a sheet from the bottom, covering about 62% of the screen so the card you tapped stays visible.
    • Drag the grip up to open it to 94%, or down to close it.
    • With the grip focused, ↑ and ↓ move it in steps.
    • Tapping the dimmed page above closes it.

Close the panel with ✕ or Esc. Focus returns to the card you opened it from.

The panel header

  • The family name, and a line with its category, by designer, number of styles, ⚡ number of variable axes (for variable fonts) and number of glyphs.
  • ＋ Select adds the family to Your kit; it then reads ✓ Selected, and selecting it again removes the family.
  • Download kit downloads a self-hosting ZIP for this family. For families that are listed but not served it reads Installed only and is disabled. See Your kit, compare and download.

Eleven tabs follow: Specimen, Suitability, Pairings, Glyphs, Features, Styles, About, License, Charset, Develop and Use & Embed. The Specimen tab is described here; the others are in Family details and Develop and embed.

The Specimen tab

A large editable specimen, set at 64 pixels in the family's regular weight. It starts with your preview text, or The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. Click into it and type anything; the placeholder reads Type here to test the typeface… when it is empty.

Bold and italic only look right if the family has those styles; otherwise the browser imitates them.

Variable axes

For a variable font, a slider appears for each axis under the specimen, labelled with its name, tag and range, for example Weight · wght · 100–900. Moving a slider changes the specimen at once. Choosing a weight from the menu also moves the weight axis.

If the font has named instances (such as Light or Condensed Bold), they appear as chips; select one to set every axis to that instance.

OpenType features

If the family has features you can switch, an OpenType features box lists them, grouped as Ligatures, Letterforms, Figures & numbers, Spacing, Stylistic sets and Character variants, and says how many there are. Select a chip to switch a feature on in the specimen; select it again to switch it off. The Features tab shows each one before and after.

Presets

Chips under the specimen replace the text with a standard sample:

PresetText
PangramThe quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog
ParagraphA paragraph about typography, for testing body text
Alphabeta to z
UppercaseA to Z
NumbersDigits, currency signs, fractions and other number signs
SymbolsAmpersand, at sign, copyright and other punctuation
CodeA short program with operators, look-alike characters and a hexadecimal number

Code also caps the size at 24 pixels, keeps the line breaks, and switches on contextual alternates and ligatures if the family has them, so you can see coding ligatures such as => and !=.
