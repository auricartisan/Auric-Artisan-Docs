Read the results

Understand the threshold readouts, the staircase trace and the explanation tabs of the Psychophysics Bench.

The readouts

ReadoutWhat it tells you
Threshold, MichelsonYour threshold contrast, four decimals; highlighted when there is not yet an estimate
At what percentageThe proportion correct the threshold belongs to
Reversals usedHow many reversals went into the estimate, out of how many occurred
Delivered / askedThe contrast the screen could show divided by the contrast the staircase asked for; 1.00 means nothing was capped
Trials runTrials completed and scored
Proportion correctCorrect trials divided by trials run, three decimals
Degrees per pixelThe angle one pixel covers at your geometry
Nyquist limit of the gridThe finest frequency your screen can show at your geometry; highlighted when the stimulus is above it

How the threshold is worked out

1-up / 2-down, 3-down / 1-up and Accelerated. The first two reversals are a warm-up and are discarded. Of the rest, an even number is used (if an odd number remain, the earliest is dropped too), because up-runs and down-runs of a transformed staircase are not symmetric around the threshold. The threshold is the geometric mean of those reversal contrasts, because the staircase steps by ratios. Reversals used reads, for example, 10 of 12; before there are two usable reversals it reads fewer than two reversals after the warm-up and the threshold shows —.

Bayesian tracker. The threshold is the tracker's current estimate (the mean of its distribution on a log scale), and Reversals used reads, for example, 80 trials, posterior mean.

Constant stimuli. There is no threshold estimate; Threshold, Michelson and Reversals used show —. Read Proportion correct: it is your score at the starting contrast.

What the percentage means

ProcedureAt what percentage
1-up / 2-down70.7%
3-down / 1-up79.4%
Bayesian tracker80.3% (two-interval) or 61.9% (yes/no)
Accelerated, Constant stimulinot defined for this ladder

A threshold from the accelerated staircase is a contrast near your threshold, but not a threshold at a stated percentage, and should not be compared directly with one.

What is a normal value?

For a Gabor at about 4 c/deg in a dim room, detection thresholds typically fall roughly between 0.005 and 0.02 Michelson contrast on a well-behaved display. A bright room, an uncalibrated screen, glare or inattention all raise it. At such low contrasts the stripes differ from the background by only a few screen code values, so screen quality matters.

The staircase trace

The staircase plots the contrast on every trial (horizontal axis: trial number; vertical axis: contrast on a logarithmic scale, labelled on the left). The note above it names the procedure.

  • Grey dots — the first two reversals, the warm-up, discarded.
  • Red dots — the reversals the estimate uses.
  • Dashed gold line — the threshold, with its value on the right.

A good run falls from the starting contrast, then zig-zags in a narrowing band. The trace is drawn for the three staircases that have reversals (1-up / 2-down, 3-down / 1-up, Accelerated). For the Bayesian tracker and Constant stimuli it stays empty with the text The trace appears here once the run starts; read the numbers instead.

Double-click the trace or the stimulus to see it enlarged; press Esc to close.

Warnings under the readouts

  • Above the pixel grid's Nyquist limit. — the frequency is too fine for your screen at your geometry; what you see is an alias.
  • The contrast asked for does not fit about this background. — the staircase asked for more contrast than fits; Delivered / asked is below 1.00. A threshold measured while this shows is a threshold for a different stimulus. Choose a darker background.

What the page does not show

The bench computes more than it displays. A psychometric function fit (a logistic with the task's guess rate and a fitted lapse rate), d′ and criterion for yes/no sessions, and a bootstrap interval are described in the Data register, but no readout on the page shows them in the current version. The session file records every trial, so you can fit them in your own analysis software.

The explanation tabs

When the tabs open (see Troubleshooting), they hold the following.

Method has three views:

  • The contrast chain — the steps from Contrast asked through Mean luminance, L × (1 ± m) and the sRGB OETF to Code values, and a table of what an earlier version delivered for contrasts of 0.02 to 1 (Contrast asked, Codes sent, Luminance reached, Contrast delivered, Error).
  • The frequency axis — a table of your current viewing distance, pixel pitch, degrees per pixel, cycles per pixel at your frequency, and the Nyquist limit, with where each comes from.
  • What each ladder estimates — each procedure and the percentage it converges at, and notes on the multiplicative step, the even reversal count and why the fit carries a guess rate.

Paradigms lists the five tasks the menu once offered: the two that score (tagged Scores) and three withdrawn ones (tagged Never correct), each with what it would take to bring back.

Data lists 13 entries with a status each: Verbatim (3: sRGB transfer, Levitt's rules, the 1/2N extreme-proportion correction), Computed (5: Michelson contrast in luminance, viewing geometry, signal-detection formulae, inverse normal, bootstrap), Stand-in (3: Bayesian tracker, psychometric fit, accelerated staircase) and Absent (2: Weibull fit, ROC curve). Its Calibrate the display area (Drop a measured gamma table) is not active in the current version.

Reference lists eleven sources, from Levitt (1971) to Abramowitz and Stegun (1964), each with what it is used for and a tag: Runs, Stand-in or Withdrawn.

You should now be able to say what your threshold is a threshold of, and how much to trust it.
