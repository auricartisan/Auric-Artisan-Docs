Read what a transform moved

Interpret the clip overlay, the colour difference cards and the hue circle on the Lab tab.

Everything below the before and after pair on the Lab tab describes the difference between the two images, for the transform currently shown: a look at its strength, or a loaded file.

The pair

  • Before is the scene, with its pixel count beside the label, for example 57600 for a 320 × 180 scene.
  • After is the scene through the transform, with the clipped share beside the label, for example 0.83% clipped.

The clip overlay

With What the transform crushed switched on, the After image is hatched with diagonal stripes where the transform clipped a pixel:

StripesMeaning
PinkA channel was driven to white (255) that was not at 255 before
BlueA channel was driven to black (0) that was not at 0 before

Pixels that were already at 0 or 255 and stayed there are not marked. Switch the overlay off to see the clean result; the figures do not change.

What it moved

CardMeaning
mean ΔE₀₀The average CIEDE2000 colour difference between each pixel before and after
95th percentileThe difference that 95% of pixels are at or below; shows how big the typical large change is
largestThe single largest difference on any pixel
clippedThe share of pixels with at least one channel driven to 0 or 255 by the transform; the card turns red above 1%
the old test saidThe share of output pixels with any channel at 0 or 255, whatever it was before

The last card is the test an earlier version of the tool used. It counts every saturated or black or white pixel in the result, even those the transform did not touch, so it is shown only for comparison. On the Colour patches or Saturation field scene, compare it with clipped to see the difference.

Where it moved things to

The hue circle tests the transform on 72 colours arranged round a ring in CIELAB, at a constant lightness (L* 60) and chroma (C* 40), one every 5° of hue.

  • The angle round the circle is the CIELAB hue; the distance from the centre is the chroma. Faint rings mark every 20 units of chroma.
  • Each hollow dot is a test colour before the transform. The filled dot, joined to it by a thin line, is where the transform moved it.
  • A pink ring around a filled dot marks a test colour the transform clipped.

Not every colour at L* 60, C* 40 fits inside sRGB. Test colours that do not fit are pulled in towards the centre, keeping their hue, until they do. The caption says how many: 13 of 72, whatever the transform, because the ring is the same every time. This is done before the transform runs, so the shift you see belongs to the transform, not to the gamut.

The circle cards

CardMeaning
largest hue shiftThe largest turn of hue on any test colour, in degrees, with its direction (+ or −)
mean hue shiftThe average size of the hue turn across all 72, in degrees
largest L shift*The largest change in CIELAB lightness, with its direction
clipped on the circleHow many of the 72 test colours the transform clipped

Hue alone rarely tells the whole story: most looks move lightness and chroma at the same time, often by more. The CSV export of the hue sweep gives all three for every test colour; see Write a .cube file.

Reading the numbers together

  • A look with a small mean ΔE₀₀ but a large largest changes most colours a little and a few a lot. Find the few with the overlay or the hue circle.
  • A high clipped figure on your own picture means detail is being lost in highlights or shadows. Lower Strength, or for the tone-map, change Tone-map exposure.
  • A consistent twist on the hue circle, all dots turning the same way, is a hue rotation. Dots moving inwards or outwards are chroma changes.

The Method tab

The Method tab explains the method in five sections, chosen with the buttons under The chain:

SectionWhat it shows
Sample orderHow a .cube indexes its rows, with the first eight rows of a 2 × 2 × 2 table
InterpolationHow trilinear interpolation works, and the worst error on an identity at sizes 2 to 65
ClippingSeven test pixels judged by the old test and by the clip test used now
Round tripWriting a table and reading it back, and whether an identity survives at various sizes
Linear lightThe tone-map applied in linear light compared with applying it to encoded values
