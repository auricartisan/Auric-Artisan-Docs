Getting started with the Collage Maker

Open the Collage Maker, find your way around its six tabs, and make and export a first collage.

Open the tool

Go to https://auricartisan.com/tool/general/tools/collage-maker/. The page opens on the Studio tab with an empty 1200 × 900 canvas. You do not need an account, and nothing needs installing.

A note under the page heading explains what the numbers on the page are measurements of. It has no close button on purpose.

The screen at a glance

The tool has six tabs.

TabWhat it is for
StudioThe working area: pictures, canvas settings, history, the canvas, layers and readout cards
LayoutEvery grid arrangement for your pictures, laid out and measured, with a button to apply each one
MeasureHow the coverage, overlap and off-canvas figures are measured, with worked examples
DataA list of every figure on the page and where it comes from; the tab shows the number of entries (12)
ExportExport scale and JPEG quality, the save, copy and session buttons, file sizes and the reading
ReferenceThe published sources the tool relies on, and the arithmetic it runs

The Studio tab

The Studio tab has two parts.

  • The left-hand panel holds, from top to bottom: Pictures (the drop box), The canvas (shape, padding, gap, corner radius and three switches), History (undo and redo), The selected picture (sliders and buttons for the picture you have selected), and two buttons: Arrange in a grid and Clear the canvas.
  • The right-hand panel holds The canvas itself, with its size above it, the Layers · top first list beside it, and a row of cards under What is on the canvas.

A chequerboard behind the canvas shows where it is transparent. The chequerboard, the guides, the selection outline and the corner handles are only on screen; none of them is ever written into an exported file.

Make a first collage

  1. On the Studio tab, drop three or four pictures onto the Drop pictures box. You can also select the box, or press Enter or Space while it has keyboard focus, to choose files. The box accepts JPG, PNG, WebP and GIF.
  2. Each picture appears in the middle of the canvas, shrunk if necessary so that it fits within 42% of the canvas width and height. The most recently added picture is selected, and all of them appear in the Layers · top first list.
  3. Open the Shape menu under The canvas and choose a shape, for example 1 : 1 — 1200 × 1200. The canvas changes size; the pictures stay where they were.
  4. Select Arrange in a grid. The tool tries every number of columns, measures how much of the canvas each arrangement covers, and applies the best one. A short message says which grid it chose and how much it covers.
  5. Click a picture on the canvas to select it. Drag it to move it. Drag one of its four corner handles to make it larger or smaller around its centre.
  6. With a picture selected, use the Rotation slider to turn it, or Flip across to mirror it.
  7. Look at the cards under What is on the canvas. canvas covered is the share of the canvas the pictures paint; outside the canvas turns into a warning when part of a picture hangs off the edge.
  8. Open the Export tab and select Save the PNG. Your browser downloads collage.png, and a message confirms the file size.

You should now have a PNG of your collage, at 1200 pixels wide, with a transparent background and rounded corners (the default corner radius is 18 px).

Next steps

  • To change the background to white or remove the rounded corners, see Build and edit a collage.
  • To choose a grid yourself rather than letting the tool pick, see Arrange pictures in a grid.
  • To export larger, as JPEG, or to save the layout for later, see Export, copy and save sessions.
