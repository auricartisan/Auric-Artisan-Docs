Collage Maker

What the Collage Maker does, who it is for, where to find it and how to make a first collage.

The Collage Maker (the page is titled Collage Studio) puts your pictures on a canvas so you can move, scale, rotate, flip and stack them, then export the result as a PNG or JPEG. You choose the shape of the canvas from six presets, round its corners, and decide whether the background is transparent or white. A grid arranger lays the pictures out for you and compares every possible grid before you commit to one.

Beside the canvas, the tool measures what you have made. It reports how much of the canvas the pictures cover, how much of it is painted twice where pictures overlap, and how much picture area hangs off the edge and will not be exported. Coverage and overlap are read from the pixels of the same image the export produces, so the figures describe the file you download.

Undo reaches back to where you started, including pictures you removed and a canvas you cleared. Pictures are read in the page and drawn straight to the canvas; nothing is uploaded.

Who it is for

  • Designers and social media editors assembling a set of photographs into one image.
  • Anyone who needs a transparent PNG composite with rounded corners.
  • People who want to know, in numbers, how much of the frame their pictures fill before they export.

Where to find it

Open https://auricartisan.com/tool/general/tools/collage-maker/ in a current desktop or mobile browser. It is listed as Collage Maker on the Collections page at https://auricartisan.com/collections/. No installation is needed.

Quick start

  1. Open the tool. The Studio tab is selected.
  2. Drop your pictures on the Drop pictures box, or select it to choose files (JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF).
  3. Pick a Shape for the canvas, for example 1 : 1 — 1200 × 1200.
  4. Select Arrange in a grid to lay the pictures out in the arrangement that covers the most canvas.
  5. Click a picture to select it, drag it to move it, and drag a corner handle to resize it.
  6. Open the Export tab and select Save the PNG. The file downloads as collage.png.

What you can do

  • Choose from six canvas shapes, all 1200 pixels wide, and set corner radius, padding and gap.
  • Add any number of pictures, and move, scale (2% to 400% by slider), rotate, fade, flip, hide, duplicate, reorder and remove each one.
  • Snap moves to a 10 px grid and show centre and rule-of-thirds guides while you work.
  • Compare every grid arrangement, fitted or filled, and apply one in a single step.
  • Undo and redo up to 60 steps, including removals and clearing the canvas.
  • Read measured coverage, overlap and off-canvas area as you work.
  • Export PNG (with transparency) or JPEG at 0.25× to 4× scale, copy a PNG to the clipboard, and save or load the layout as a session file.
  • Save a short JSON reading of what was measured.
