Explore ICtCp and Jzazbz planes

View the three gamuts in the ICtCp or Jzazbz perceptual planes, read the boundary rings from 0.1 to 10,000 nits, and convert HEX colours.

Key ideas

  • ICtCp (ITU-R BT.2100, from Dolby) splits colour into I (intensity), Ct (tritan, blue–yellow) and Cp (protan, red–green). It converts linear Rec. 2020 RGB to cone responses, encodes each with the PQ curve, then rotates to I, Ct, Cp. It is the basis of the ΔE ITP colour difference.
  • Jzazbz (Safdar et al., 2017) gives lightness Jz and opponent axes az and bz. It starts from absolute XYZ and uses its own PQ-like quantiser with the exponent raised by a factor of 1.7 (p = 134.034375).
  • Both are built to keep equal steps roughly equal from very dark to very bright, which CIELAB does not.

View the plane

  1. Set the luminance first: on the Curves tab, choose a tone curve, peak brightness and Signal level. Together they define the absolute luminance the plane is drawn at.
  2. Open the Planes tab.
  3. Under Perceptual plane, choose ICtCp (Ct–Cp) (default) or JzAzBz (Az–Bz). The title and note change to name the space and its quantiser.
  4. Read the plane: each gamut switched on in the Lab's Overlay gamuts is outlined, showing how far its chroma extends at that luminance.
  5. Read Boundary rings by luminance: the same boundaries at eight luminances — 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 500, 1,000, 4,000 and 10,000 nits — listed under Boundary rings in the rail with a matching key. A ring is a slice at one luminance, so the stack shows the boundary changing with brightness.

The Tone mapping operator menu (Reinhard, Hable (Filmic), ACES Filmic, Uchimura (Gran Turismo)) is shown on this tab, but in the current version it does not change any plot. For tone mapping, use the Tone Mapping lab.

Convert a list of colours

  1. Set the tone curve, peak and Signal level on the Curves tab; the conversion uses the resulting luminance.
  2. Open Export and find Convert a list.
  3. Enter six-digit HEX colours, one per line or separated by commas.
  4. Select Convert to ICtCp. The table lists Hex, L*, a*, b*, I, Ct, Cp, Jz, Az and Bz for each colour.
  5. Select Download the result to copy the table as CSV to your clipboard (the button copies rather than downloads).

The ICtCp and Jzazbz values depend on the luminance you set. Treat them as illustrations of how the spaces behave; see Limits and accuracy.

Export the plane

On Export, choose The plane (PNG) to download the ICtCp or Jzazbz plane at the current luminance.

Result

You can see how much chroma each broadcast gamut offers at a given HDR luminance, in a space designed for HDR, and how that changes from 0.1 to 10,000 nits.
