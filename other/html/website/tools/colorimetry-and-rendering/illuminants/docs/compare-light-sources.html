Compare two light sources

Overlay two illuminants, read their CCT, CRI and white points side by side, and judge metamerism risk with the surface samples.

Use this when you need to explain why a product, print or swatch looks different in two places — for example daylight at a window against tungsten at home, or a D50 viewing booth against a D65 screen.

Before you start

  • Metamerism is when two surfaces match under one light and differ under another. The lab estimates the risk between two lights with a metamerism index: the average CIEDE2000 colour difference of 14 test samples rendered under each light. Bigger means more risk.
  • CRI (colour rendering index) scores how faithfully a light renders test colours compared with a reference light of the same CCT (a blackbody below 5,000 K, CIE daylight at or above). Ra is the mean of the first eight scores; R9 is saturated red.
  • In this lab the test samples are approximations of the CIE 13.3 set, not the published reflectances. CRI and the metamerism index are therefore indicative. See Limits and accuracy.

Steps

  1. Open the Lab tab.
  2. Under Illuminant, choose the first light, for example CIE D65 (6504 K) — Daylight, sRGB.
  3. Under Comparison illuminant, tick Enable comparison.
  4. Open the comparison list and choose the second light. The choices are CIE A, D50, D55, D65, D75, equal-energy E, F2, F7 and F11. The default is CIE A (2856 K).
  5. Look at the SPD chart. The gold curve is your first light and the blue curve is the comparison light. Both are drawn on one relative scale.
  6. Scroll to Illuminant comparison. The table shows, for each light:
    • CCT in kelvin;
    • CRI Ra;
    • CIE x, y;
    • and, across both, Metamerism Index (avg ΔE₀₀).
  7. Under Surface reflectance samples, turn on the surfaces you care about. The choices are White, Gray, Red, Green, Blue, Skin, Yellow and Cyan. White, Gray, Red, Green and Skin are on by default.
  8. Read Surface appearance — D65 reference vs test illuminant. Each row shows two swatches (under D65, then under your first light after adaptation), both HEX values and a ΔE₀₀ figure.
  9. Check the CRI chart for the first light. Look at R9 separately; Ra can hide weak red rendering.

Reading the result

  • A metamerism index below about 0.5 suggests very low risk; 0.5 to 2 is moderate; 2 to 4 is high; above 4 means a visible shift is almost certain. These bands come from the lab's own research notes (Reference › Research notes) and are a rule of thumb, not a standard.
  • The surface rows answer a slightly different question: after your eye has adapted to the light, how far does each surface's colour move compared with how it looks in daylight?
  • A light can have a similar white point to D65 and still render surfaces differently. That is the spectrum at work, and it is why the lab shows the spectrum and not only the white.

Tips

  • To study a fluorescent source, choose F2, F7 or F11 as the first light and D65 as the comparison. Remember that the fluorescent spectra are stand-ins; the chart labels them so.
  • To see the full list of 18 illuminants side by side, open Export and select Generate comparison table. It lists CCT, x, y, CRI Ra and a white swatch for each.
  • To compare many lights by x, y, CCT, Duv, efficacy or spectral spread, use the query console.

Result

You have a side-by-side table of two lights, a metamerism index, and a row of surface swatches showing how much each material shifts between them.
