Getting started

Your first hour

A guided first hour on the website — finding your way, a first contrast check, a colour ramp with tokens, a page audit, vision simulation, saving work and setting preferences.

This tour takes about an hour and needs no account. Each stop names the controls as they appear on screen, says roughly how long it takes, and ends with what you should see. Links point to the full documentation for each tool.

Stop 1 — Find your way around (5 minutes)

  1. Open https://auricartisan.com/. On your very first visit a short welcome animation plays; press Esc, Enter or Space, or click, to skip it.
  2. When the cookie bar appears at the bottom, choose Accept All, Reject All or Customise. Every tool works whichever you pick. See Privacy basics.
  3. Look at the header. From left to right it gives you the home link, Search, and the Company, Tools and Learn menus, followed by icon buttons for the app, the browser extension, VS Code, search, the guide, the library, language and theme, and finally Sign in and Create account.
  4. Press Ctrl + K and type "contrast". Open the Contrast Checker from the results, then come back.
  5. Press ? (while not typing in a field) to open the Guide. It offers quick paths such as Run a quick audit, Build color assets, Check accessibility and Find a feature fast. Close it with Esc.

Result: you can reach any tool from search, the menus or the guide.

Stop 2 — A first contrast check (5 minutes)

  1. On the home page, scroll to Pick two colours, watch the verdict move.
  2. Choose a Text swatch and a Background swatch.
  3. Read the contrast ratio and the Normal text and Large text verdicts (AA, AAA or fail).
  4. Select Open the full checker to continue in the Contrast Checker with your own colours.

Result: you know whether a colour pair is readable, and why. WCAG (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) sets 4.5:1 as the AA minimum for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Full guide: Contrast Checker.

Stop 3 — One brand colour to a token file (10 minutes)

  1. On the home page, find One brand color to a shippable system.
  2. Enter your brand colour in Brand colour (or type a hex value in the field beside it).
  3. Watch the three steps update: Accessible ramp (nine steps measured against the page surface), Vision check (the ramp through colour-vision models) and Export tokens.
  4. Under Export tokens, choose CSS, Tailwind or JSON, then select Copy tokens.
  5. Paste the tokens into your project.

Result: a readable colour scale in a format your build reads. For a complete system (type, spacing, radius, shadow and motion as well as colour), continue in the Design System Generator.

Stop 4 — Audit a web page (15 minutes)

  1. Open Analyze URLs from Tools › Core tools (https://auricartisan.com/tool/analyzer/).
  2. Paste the address of a page you own or are allowed to test. The Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy only permit analysis of sites you own or are authorised to test.
  3. Run the audit and stay in Simple mode for your first run.
  4. Work through Overview, then the fixes, then the report, as the Guide's "Run a quick audit" path suggests.
  5. Export a report if you want to keep one.

Result: a list of accessibility and colour issues on the page, with suggested fixes. Runs that need Auric Artisan's servers, such as whole-site crawls, show their token cost before you start and need an account. Full guide: Analyzer.

Stop 5 — See it through other eyes (10 minutes)

  1. Open Vision Simulation (https://auricartisan.com/tool/vision-simulation/).
  2. Load an image or screenshot of your design.
  3. Switch between protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia and check that colour-coded information is still distinguishable.

Result: you can spot colour pairs that collapse for people with colour-vision deficiency. The simulations are models for design and learning, not a medical test. Full guide: Vision Simulation.

Stop 6 — Save and find your work (5 minutes)

  1. In any tool you used, use its save or export control to keep a palette, gradient or report.
  2. Open Library from the header (the button described as "Saved palettes and exports").
  3. Find what you saved.

Result: your work is kept in My Library in this browser. It stays on this device and is not uploaded unless you publish it. See Library and Library Kit.

Stop 7 — Make the site yours (5 minutes)

  1. Press Ctrl + , to open Settings.
  2. Under Appearance, choose a Theme (Light, Dark or System), an accent colour, a Language and a Text size.
  3. Under Comfort, try Reduce motion, Boost contrast or Reading mode (Alt + R).
  4. Close with Esc.

Result: the whole site follows your preferences on this device. See Light and dark theme, Language and Settings panel.

Stop 8 — Optional: create an account (5 minutes)

If you want to publish a portfolio, run server-backed crawls, or keep a paid plan across devices, create a free account: Create an account (optional). Then open the dashboard at https://auricartisan.com/auth/dashboard/ and follow Finish setting up in the side column.

Where to go after the first hour

  • Learn the theory behind what you just did: the Learning Hub at https://auricartisan.com/library/learn/.
  • Work on any website you visit: Browser extension.
  • Work in your editor: VS Code extensions.
  • Use the site without a connection: Offline use.
