Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the general, accessibility and colour-science terms used across Auric Artisan's tools and documentation.

Terms used across the website, the extensions and these docs. Tool-specific terms are defined again in each tool's own glossary. Account and billing terms are in Account and billing glossary; legal terms are in Company and legal glossary.

General and site terms

Accent colour — The highlight colour the site uses for buttons and marks. Auric gold by default; you can change it in Settings.

API — Application programming interface: a way for your own code to use Auric Artisan's functions over the internet. The REST API is metered in API credits.

Cache — Copies of pages and files your browser keeps so they load faster or work offline.

Collection — A named group of saved items in your library.

Design tokens — Named values (colours, sizes, spacing) exported in a format a build system reads, such as JSON, so a design system can be shared between design and code.

CSS custom properties — Variables in CSS, written like --color-primary, that design tokens are often exported as.

Export — Saving a result out of a tool as a file (PNG, SVG, PDF, JSON, CSS and so on) or as copied text.

Kit — A floating panel that opens over any page, such as the Library Kit.

My Library — Your saved palettes, gradients, reports and exports, kept in your browser.

PWA — Progressive web app: a website your browser can install like an app and keep available offline.

Service worker — The part of a website that runs in the background of your browser to manage the offline cache.

Token (tool token) — The unit that pays for server-backed work you start in a website tool, such as a whole-site crawl. Not the same as a design token.

Accessibility terms

AA and AAA — WCAG conformance levels. For text contrast, AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; AAA requires 7:1 and 4.5:1.

APCA — Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm: a newer contrast measure that reports a lightness contrast value (Lc) and accounts for text size and polarity (dark on light versus light on dark) better than the WCAG ratio.

ARIA — Accessible Rich Internet Applications: attributes that tell assistive technology what an interactive element is and what state it is in.

Assistive technology — Tools such as screen readers, magnifiers and switch devices that help people use computers.

Contrast ratio — The WCAG measure of how different two colours are in brightness, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white), calculated from their relative luminance.

Focus indicator — The visible outline that shows which element the keyboard is on.

Large text — In WCAG, text at least 18 point, or 14 point bold.

Screen reader — Software that reads the screen aloud or to a braille display.

Touch target — The area you tap. WCAG 2.2 asks for at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels; its enhanced level asks for 44 by 44.

WCAG — Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the W3C's standard for accessible web content. Versions 2.1 and 2.2 are referred to across the site.

Colour vision

Achromatopsia — Absence of working cone cells, leaving vision without colour.

Anomalous trichromacy — All three cone types are present but one responds abnormally, weakening some colour distinctions. Protanomaly, deuteranomaly and tritanomaly affect the long-, medium- and short-wavelength cones.

Colour vision deficiency (CVD) — Reduced ability to tell some colours apart; often called colour blindness.

Cone cells — The light-sensitive cells in the eye that see colour. There are three kinds: L (long wavelength, reddish), M (medium, greenish) and S (short, bluish).

Dichromacy — One cone type is missing. Protanopia (no L cones), deuteranopia (no M cones) and tritanopia (no S cones).

Ishihara plate — A pattern of coloured dots hiding a number, used to screen for colour-vision deficiency. Auric Artisan's generator makes Ishihara-style plates for learning only.

Vision simulation — A model that shows approximately how an image looks with a given colour-vision deficiency or eye condition. Auric Artisan uses the Viénot 1999, Brettel 1997 and Machado 2009 models.

Colour science

Chroma — How colourful a colour is compared with a grey of the same lightness.

Chromatic adaptation — The eye's adjustment to the colour of the light, so a white page looks white under warm or cool light. Transforms such as Von Kries, Bradford, CIECAT02 and CAT16 model it.

CIE — The International Commission on Illumination, which publishes the standards behind most colour science.

CIE XYZ — The 1931 colour space derived from human colour-matching experiments; the reference most other spaces are defined from.

CIELAB (L*a*b*) — A colour space designed so that equal distances look roughly equally different: L* is lightness, a* runs green to red, b* runs blue to yellow.

CIELUV — A related uniform space often used for light sources and displays.

CMYK — Cyan, magenta, yellow and black ink; the colour model of print. Conversions from screen colours are approximations.

Colour space — A system of numbers for describing colours, such as sRGB, Display P3 or CIELAB.

Delta E (ΔE) — A number for how different two colours look. Formulas include CIE76, CIE94, CIEDE2000 (ΔE00, the current standard) and CMC l:c. As a rough guide, a ΔE00 under 1 is not noticeable.

Display P3 — A wide-gamut colour space used by many modern screens; it holds more saturated colours than sRGB.

Gamut — The range of colours a device or colour space can show.

Gamut mapping — Bringing colours that are outside a target gamut into it while keeping them looking as close as possible.

HDR — High dynamic range: images and displays with a wider range of brightness than standard.

HSL, HSV (HSB), HWB — Cylindrical ways of writing sRGB colours by hue plus saturation and lightness, value (brightness), or whiteness and blackness. Easy to use, but not perceptually uniform.

Hue — The attribute that makes a colour red, yellow, green, blue and so on, often given as an angle.

ICC profile — A standard file that describes how a device or colour space reproduces colour, used by colour management.

Illuminant — A standard description of a light source's spectrum. D65 is average daylight; D50 is used in print; A is incandescent light; F2 and F11 are fluorescent lights.

LCH / OKLCH — The polar forms of CIELAB and Oklab: lightness, chroma and hue.

Lightness — How light a colour looks, independent of its hue.

LUT — Look-up table: a file (commonly .cube) that maps input colours to output colours, used for colour grading.

Luminance (relative) — How bright a colour is, weighted by the eye's sensitivity; the basis of the WCAG contrast ratio.

Metamerism — Two colours that match under one light but not under another, because their spectra differ.

Munsell system — A colour-order system describing colours by hue, value and chroma, used as a physical reference.

Oklab — A modern perceptually uniform colour space (Björn Ottosson, 2020) with steadier hue as lightness changes; used for smooth palettes and scales.

Perceptually uniform — A colour space in which equal numeric steps look like equal visual steps.

Rendering intent — The rule used when gamut mapping, for example keeping colours exact where possible or keeping their relationships.

Spectral power distribution — How much light a source or surface gives off at each wavelength.

sRGB — The standard colour space of the web and most consumer screens.

Standard observer — The CIE's average human colour-matching response, measured for a 2° or 10° field of view.

Tonal scale (ramp) — A series of tints and shades of one colour, such as steps 50 to 950, ideally spaced evenly to the eye.

Tone mapping — Compressing HDR brightness into the range a standard display can show.

White point — The colour a space or device treats as white, such as D65.

Colour harmony

Analogous — Colours next to each other on the colour wheel.

Complementary — Colours opposite each other on the wheel.

Split-complementary — A colour plus the two neighbours of its complement.

Triadic — Three colours evenly spaced around the wheel.

Tetradic (square) — Four colours in two complementary pairs, or evenly spaced.

Perception research

Psychophysics — Measuring how physical stimuli (such as colour differences) relate to what people perceive, usually through controlled experiments.
