# Comfort options

The **Comfort** section holds switches that make the site easier to read and calmer to use. Each switch takes effect at once and applies to every page. Open it with `Ctrl` + `,`, then select **Comfort**.

## Reading mode

**Reading mode** gives a distraction-free layout with comfortable typography.

While it is on:

- floating chrome is hidden: the kit launchers, the right-edge menu strip, the info bars and the clock strip;
- the custom cursor is replaced by your system cursor;
- the main text sits in a narrower, centred column with larger type and more generous line spacing;
- the header no longer sticks to the top of the screen;
- a thin bar across the very top of the window fills as you scroll down the page;
- a **Reading mode ✕** chip appears near the bottom of the screen. Select it to leave reading mode.

Ways to switch it on or off:

- the **Reading mode** switch in Comfort;
- `Alt` + `R` anywhere on the site (not while typing in a field);
- right-click the page, choose **View**, then **Reading mode** (or **Leave reading mode**);
- on card pages (home, collections, blog, changelog, documentation, learn), right-click a card and select the **Reading** pill.

A short message ("Reading mode on" or "Reading mode off") confirms a change made from the right-click menu.

## Reduce motion

**Reduce motion** minimises animations and transitions across the site. Animations finish instantly, smooth scrolling becomes an instant jump, and the rotating info bars swap messages without fading.

Toggle it with `Alt` + `M` anywhere on the site. A message confirms "Reduced motion on" or "Reduced motion off".

> **Note:** This switch is the site's own setting. The site also respects your operating system's reduced-motion preference: with that on, the custom cursor is not shown and the eye-rest horizon does not animate, whatever this switch says.

## Reduce transparency

**Reduce transparency** turns off blur and translucency, so panels and overlays are drawn as solid surfaces. This can make text over panels clearer and can help on slower devices.

## Boost contrast

**Boost contrast** strengthens secondary text and borders for easier reading. Muted text is drawn in the stronger secondary text colour, and borders use a darker line.

## Underline links

**Underline links** always underlines links inside articles and prose: paragraphs, list items, definitions, quotations and captions. Navigation menus and buttons are not changed.

## Custom cursor

**Custom cursor** controls the ring and dot that follow your pointer. It is on by default. Switch it off to use your system cursor everywhere; the change is immediate, with no reload. See [Custom cursor](../../custom-cursor/README.md).

## Eye-rest reminders

**Eye-rest reminders** are gentle reminders to rest your eyes on a schedule. They are off by default.

1. Switch on **Eye-rest reminders**.
2. Under **Reminder interval** (minutes between reminders), select **20m**, **35m**, **45m** or **60m**. The default is 20 minutes.

The interval buttons are dimmed while the switch is off. Changing the interval restarts the countdown at once. The reminder itself, and its own options, are described in [Eye rest](../../eye-rest/README.md).

## Screen saver

**Screen saver** shows an ambient colour screen after a period of inactivity. It is off by default.

1. Switch on **Screen saver**.
2. Under **Start after** (minutes of inactivity), select **1m**, **5m**, **10m**, **20m** or **30m**. The default is 10 minutes.

The delay buttons are dimmed while the switch is off. Switching the screen saver off while it is showing closes it at once. See [Screen saver](../../screen-saver/README.md).

## Restore the section

Select **Restore section defaults** to return every Comfort switch and both timings to their defaults: everything off except **Custom cursor**, a 20-minute reminder interval and a 10-minute screen saver delay. A message confirms "Comfort restored to defaults".
