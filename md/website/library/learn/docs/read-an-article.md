# Read an article

Every Learn article follows the same layout. This page walks through it from top to bottom.

## The header

- A small line reading **Auric Artisan · Learn** above the title.
- The title and an introductory paragraph.
- A row of facts, each with a bold label: the article's category and its number in the course (for example "Physics · 112"), how many live demos it has, the reading time (for example "~30 min"), and its subject.
- Three actions:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Download** | Opens a small menu: **Markdown** (".md — Formatted, portable text") or **Plain text** (".txt — Just the words"). The file is made in your browser from the article's text; interactive demos, figures and the quiz are left out. |
| **Print** | Opens your browser's print dialog. |
| **← All Articles** | Returns to the Learn page. |

Downloaded files are named after the article, without the date, for example `the-color-of-heat.md`. Each starts with the title, the introduction and a "Source:" line with the article's address. Press `Escape` to close the **Download** menu.

## The table of contents

A **Table of Contents** lists the article's numbered sections. Select an entry to jump to it. A reading-progress bar at the top of the window fills as you scroll.

## Sections

Articles are divided into numbered sections, typically: an introduction, several teaching sections each with a live demo, **The terms, defined** (a glossary), **What it means for color** (practical takeaways), **Test your understanding**, and **Continue your journey**. Sections fade in as you reach them.

## Live demos

Each interactive lab appears in the article as a wide button labelled **Live demo**, with the demo's name and **Expand**.

1. Select the button. The demo opens in a panel over the page, marked **Live**, **Article** and **Dockable**.
2. Use the demo's own controls — sliders, buttons, colour pickers — and read the explanation beside it.
3. Use the panel buttons:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Dock panel** | Docks the panel to the side so you can read the article beside the demo. Select again to undock. |
| **Full screen** | Enlarges the panel to fill the window. Select again to restore. |
| **Close** | Closes the panel. `Escape` does the same. |

Docked and full screen are exclusive: turning one on turns the other off.

Demos run entirely in your browser and do not need an account.

## Test your understanding

Near the end, **Quick check** asks multiple-choice questions one at a time — six in recent articles. Choose an answer to see feedback, then select **Next →**. A counter shows your place, for example **Question 1 of 6**.

## Continue your journey

The last section suggests what to read next. On articles in the catalogue, the page replaces the author's fixed suggestions with personalised ones, and a line above them says, for example, **Tailored to Physics · You've read 12 of 113 in the library**. See [Continue your journey](continue-your-journey.md).

## The foot of the article

A line gives the article's place in the course, for example **Article 112 of 113**.

## Keeping your place

As you scroll, the article records how far you got, in this browser. Once you pass 55% of the page, the article counts as read on the Learn page and in **Continue your journey**. Reaching the bottom is not required.

## Result

You can read, explore and test yourself on any article, print it or keep a copy, and move on to a suggestion that fits what you have read.
