==============================================================================
AURIC ACCESSIBILITY GLOSSARY
==============================================================================
Accessibility terms used in the Auric Accessibility documentation.
Area: VS Code extensions › Auric Artisan Studio › Auric Accessibility   Updated: 2026-09-25

Accessibility tree. The structure the browser gives assistive technology: each
element's role, name, state and relationships.

Accessible name. What assistive technology announces for an element, from its
text, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, `alt`, `title` or a `<label>`.

ACT-aligned. Structured like the W3C's Accessibility Conformance Testing rules
format, without claiming ACT conformance.

APCA and Lc. The contrast model proposed for the WCAG 3 draft, and its
lightness contrast value. Experimental in the Studio.

ARIA. Attributes such as `role`, `aria-label` and `aria-expanded` that
describe elements to assistive technology. Checked against WAI-ARIA 1.2.

Assisted. A finding a person should confirm; it weighs less in the score.

Audit profile. The WCAG version targeted: 2.0, 2.1, 2.2 or 3.0 draft.

Autofill token. A value of the `autocomplete` attribute, such as `email` or
`current-password`, from the HTML autofill vocabulary.

Best-practice hint. A rule that flags a pattern worth fixing that does not by
itself fail WCAG.

Component. A capitalised, dotted or namespaced tag in JSX, Vue, Svelte or
Astro, such as `<Button>`, which is not judged as a native element.

Confidence. How sure a rule is that its finding is real: high, medium or low.

Impact. How much a finding affects users: critical, serious, moderate or
minor.

Inspect. The hover card that shows an element's or rule's name, role, colours,
contrast and issues.

Landmark. A region such as `main`, `nav`, `header` or `footer` that assistive
technology can jump between.

Large text. At least 24 CSS pixels, or 18.66 pixels and bold; it has a lower
contrast target.

Level. A success criterion's conformance level: A, AA or AAA. Most policies
require A and AA.

Manual check. A procedure a person carries out for what automation cannot
decide.

Role. What kind of thing an element is, such as a button, link or heading,
either from its tag or from `role`.

Success criterion. A single testable WCAG requirement, such as 1.1.1 Non-text
Content.

Suppression. Removing a finding with an `auric-disable-…` comment.

WCAG. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the W3C's accessibility
standard.

For all other terms, see the Studio glossary [../../others/glossary.txt].
