==============================================================================
SCORES AND GRADES
==============================================================================
How the overall, contrast, accessibility and code-health scores and their
letter grades are calculated, with a worked example.
Area: VS Code extensions › Auric Artisan Studio   Updated: 2026-09-25

Every score in the Studio is a number from 0 to 100, where 100 means nothing
was found. The formulas are fixed, so two projects with the same score are
comparable on what was scanned. Scores are a way to prioritise work and track
progress. They are not an accessibility certification: static checks cover
only part of WCAG, and manual testing is still needed.

THE LETTER GRADE
================

  Grade | Score
  ------+-----------
  A+    | 95 or more
  A     | 90 to 94
  B     | 80 to 89
  C     | 70 to 79
  D     | 55 to 69
  F     | below 55

The same scale is used for the overall score, the code-health score and the
brand audit.

THE OVERALL SCORE
=================

The overall score is the one shown in the status bar, the Overview ring and
the Studio's Home.

  - When the project has both resolvable contrast pairs and markup or CSS
    files, the overall score is the average of the contrast and accessibility
    sub-scores, rounded.
  - When only one of them applies, the overall score is that one.
  - When neither applies, there is no score and the grade shows `—`.

Code health is scored separately and does not change the overall score.

THE CONTRAST SUB-SCORE
======================

Every contrast pair the scanner resolved earns credit, measured against its
own target (3:1 for large text and 4.5:1 otherwise, or 4.5:1 and 7:1 at the
AAA threshold):

  The pair…                            | Credit
  -------------------------------------+-----------------
  meets AAA for its text size          | 1.00
  meets the selected target            | 0.95
  is below the target but at least 3:1 | 0.55
  is below 3:1                         | ratio ÷ 3 × 0.35

A pair graded against an assumed page background (the rule sets only `color:`
and the background was taken from `body`, `html` or `:root`) counts half,
because it is an inference rather than a reading. Every other pair counts
fully.

The contrast sub-score is the weighted average credit × 100, minus a
colour-vision penalty: the share of pairs that pass for typical vision but
weaken under colour-vision deficiency, × 25, capped at 10 points. Pairs
classified as intentional branding are left out entirely. If there are no
resolvable pairs, the contrast sub-score is omitted.

THE ACCESSIBILITY (WCAG) SUB-SCORE
==================================

Each WCAG finding adds a penalty:

  Factor             | Values
  -------------------+---------------------------------------------
  Impact             | critical 6, serious 4, moderate 2, minor 0.7
  Confidence         | high × 1, medium × 0.65, low × 0.35
  Automation         | automated × 1, assisted × 0.55, manual × 0
  Best-practice hint | × 0.5

The penalties are added up and divided by the square root of the number of
markup and CSS files scanned, so a large project is not punished merely for
its size. The sub-score is 100 minus that result, never below 0. A project
with no findings scores 100. If no markup or CSS files were scanned, the
sub-score is omitted.

Every finding counts, including repeats of the same success criterion.
Suppressed findings are removed before scoring. Branding marks never remove
WCAG findings.

THE CODE-HEALTH SCORE
=====================

Each code-health finding adds a penalty by category, scaled by confidence:

  Category         | Weight
  -----------------+-------
  Possible secrets | 14
  Security risks   | 12
  Merge markers    | 10
  Risky APIs       | 4
  Debug leftovers  | 2
  Oversized        | 0.5
  Task markers     | 0.25

  Confidence | Multiplier
  -----------+-----------
  high       | 1
  medium     | 0.75
  low        | 0.4

The total is divided by the number of files scanned, multiplied by 16, and
subtracted from 100 (never below 0). The code-health score uses the same
letter grades.

A WORKED EXAMPLE
================

A project has 10 contrast pairs and 5 markup files.

  1. Contrast: 6 pairs meet AAA (6 × 1.00), 2 meet AA (2 × 0.95), 1 is 3.2:1
     (0.55) and 1 is 2.1:1 (2.1 ÷ 3 × 0.35 = 0.245). The average credit is
     8.695 ÷ 10, so 86.95. One pair of the ten is a colour-vision risk: 0.1 ×
     25 = 2.5 points off. The contrast sub-score is 84.
  2. Accessibility: one serious, high-confidence, automated finding (4), two
     minor findings (0.7 each) and one best-practice hint (0.7 × 0.35 × 0.55 ×
     0.5, about 0.07). The total is about 5.47. Divided by √5 (about 2.24)
     that is about 2.45, so the sub-score is 98.
  3. Overall: (84 + 98) ÷ 2 = 91, grade A.

COLOURS USED FOR SCORES
=======================

  - Status bar: the score item uses the theme's warning background below 90
    and its error background below 70.
  - Studio: scores of 90 and above use the theme's pass colour, 70 to 89 the
    warning colour, and below 70 the fail colour. Every score is also written
    as a number and grade, so colour is never the only signal.

PRIORITY IN HOVERS
==================

Issue hovers show Priority N/100 with a segmented meter and one of four pieces
of advice: fix now (78 or more), fix soon (55 to 77), worth fixing (30 to 54)
or low urgency (below 30). Priority is not part of any score. It starts from
the finding's impact (critical 94, serious 78, moderate 56, minor 32), is
scaled by confidence, and for contrast findings by how far the pair falls
short of its target. Branding findings always show a low priority because they
are outside the score.
