==============================================================================
ANALYSE THE COLLECTION
==============================================================================
Use the Harmony, Accessibility and Stats tabs to see how methods, hues,
contrast and lightness are distributed across a sample of palettes.
Area: Website › Library › Colour libraries › Palette Library   Updated: 2026-09-25

The Harmony, Accessibility and Stats tabs describe many palettes at once. A
browser cannot count five million palettes, so each tab measures a sample and
says how big it was.

THE SAMPLE
==========

Every tab opens with a line such as:

  | Measured on 2,000 palettes from the start of the collection. Five million
  | cannot be counted in a browser, so these are a sample — the API samples the
  | same way.

  - No filter or search: the sample is the first 2,000 palettes of the
    collection.
  - A filter or search is on: the page looks through up to 40,000 palettes
    from the start of the collection and keeps up to 2,000 that match. The
    line then says, for example, Measured on 812 palettes matching this
    filter, out of 40,000 derived.
  - An id is in the search box: the sample is that one palette.

The sample is rebuilt every time you change a filter or search, so the tabs
always describe the filter the grid is showing. If nothing in the looked-at
stretch matches, the tabs show No palettes and suggest widening the filter.

HARMONY TAB
===========

Four cards, each a bar chart with counts:

  Card:           Generation method
  What it counts: How many sampled palettes carry each method label.

  Card:           Dominant hue bucket
  What it counts: How many have each dominant hue: red, orange, yellow, green,
                  cyan, blue, purple or pink.

  Card:           Harmony classification
  What it counts: How many are classified Neutral, Triadic, Analogous or
                  Monochrome from their measured hues (see Inspect a palette
                  [inspect-a-palette.txt]).

  Card:           Lightness & chroma profile
  What it counts: How many are Dark (L < 0.4)**, **Mid (0.4-0.7)** or **Light
                  (> 0.7), and Muted (< 0.07)**, **Balanced** or **Vivid (>
                  0.15).

ACCESSIBILITY TAB
=================

Palette-level WCAG summary
--------------------------

  Row:     Has any AA pair (4.5+)
  Meaning: Palettes with at least one pair of colours at 4.5:1 or more, as a
           count and percentage.

  Row:     Has any AAA pair (7+)
  Meaning: Palettes with at least one pair at 7:1 or more.

  Row:     Largest mutually-legible set
  Meaning: The most colours from one palette that all reach 4.5:1 against each
           other, for example 2 of 5 (sRGB allows 3).

Pair-level WCAG breakdown
-------------------------

Every palette has ten pairs, so the sample has ten times as many pairs as
palettes. The card counts AAA pairs (>= 7:1), AA pairs (>= 4.5:1) and Below
AA.

How many of the five can be used together
-----------------------------------------

This card answers a practical question: how many of a palette's five colours
can all be used as text on each other? The page explains why the answer can
never be five. Contrast depends only on how light each colour is, and between
black and white there is room for at most three lightness levels that are each
4.5:1 apart — and only two at 7:1. So no five-colour palette can have all ten
pairs at AA, here or anywhere. The card shows how many sampled palettes allow
1, 2 or 3 mutually legible colours, marking 3 as (the ceiling).

The most usable in this sample
------------------------------

Up to 24 palettes that contain at least one legible pair, ordered by their
strongest pair. The page notes that two is what this collection realistically
offers: a legible trio needs a near-black, a mid-tone and a near-white to turn
up together in one palette, which the page reports did not happen in any of
the 60,000 palettes it measured. The cards work like cards in the grid: select
one to inspect it, select a swatch to copy it.

STATS TAB
=========

A line first states how many sampled palettes contain a pair at 4.5:1 or
better, "so they can carry body text on themselves". Then four histograms:

  Card                    | Range            | Bands
  ------------------------+------------------+------
  Avg lightness (OKLCH L) | 0 to 1           | 10
  Avg chroma (OKLCH C)    | 0 to 0.30        | 10
  Hue spread              | 0 to 360 degrees | 12
  Best pair contrast      | 1:1 to 15:1      | 14

Values beyond a histogram's range are counted in its last band. The best pair
is charted rather than the worst because, as the page explains, with five
colours some pair is nearly always close to identical, so the worst pair says
little about whether a palette can carry text.

KEEPING THE TABS CURRENT
========================

Each tab is drawn when you open it and redrawn after any change of filter or
search. If a tab looks out of date, select it again.

RESULT
======

You can see, for any filter, what share of palettes can carry text, which
methods and hues dominate, and how light and colourful the palettes tend to be
— with the size of the sample stated every time.
