==============================================================================
READ A COLOUR
==============================================================================
Everything on The colour tab: the contrast verdict, the colour-blindness
values, every notation and metric, and the nearest neighbours.
Area: Website › Library › Colour libraries › Color Library   Updated: 2026-09-25

The colour tab shows the full record of one colour. It puts the verdict first
— what the colour can carry as text — then the evidence, then the raw numbers.

OPEN A COLOUR
=============

Any of these opens a colour on The colour tab:

  - select a tile in the grid, or focus it and press `Enter` or `Space`;
  - select one of the five neighbour cells under Start from a colour you
    already have;
  - select Open a random colour;
  - select a tile on the Saved tab.

The tab switches straight away; the full record loads a moment later. If you
open the tab before choosing a colour, it says No colour open with the hint
Pick one from the atlas, or paste a hex above to find its neighbours.

THE IDENTITY BLOCK
==================

At the top left:

  - a large swatch of the colour;
  - its hex code in capitals, for example #76CDF6;
  - a line with Atlas id and the id number, followed by the colour's labels
    (emotion, art movement, mood, design usage and design tag), separated by
    dots.

The block also shows Copy and Save buttons. To copy values or save the colour,
use the Export tab or the star on the colour's tile; see Troubleshooting
[troubleshooting.txt] if these two buttons do not respond.

WHAT IT CAN CARRY
=================

Two specimens show the sentence "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog."
written in the colour:

  - on a white background, with the contrast ratio and the words on white;
  - on a black background, with the ratio and on black.

Ratios of 4.5:1 or more are marked as passing, lower ratios as failing.

Under the specimens, one sentence sums the colour up:

  Situation: 4.5:1 or more on both
  Sentence:  Rare: it carries body text on either ground.

  Situation: 4.5:1 or more on white only
  Sentence:  A dark-on-light colour. Use it for text on a light page; on black
             it disappears.

  Situation: 4.5:1 or more on black only
  Sentence:  A light-on-dark colour. It is not a body-text colour on a light
             page and no weight will make it one — but on black it is one of
             the strongest in the atlas.

  Situation: Below 4.5:1 on both
  Sentence:  It carries body text on neither white nor black. This is a colour
             to draw with, not to write in.

UNDER OTHER EYES
================

Four chips show the colour as it appears to different kinds of colour vision:

  Chip          | Note                 | Meaning
  --------------+----------------------+----------------------------------
  Normal vision | the colour as stored | The colour itself.
  Protanopia    | red-blind            | No working red-sensitive cones.
  Deuteranopia  | green-blind          | No working green-sensitive cones.
  Tritanopia    | blue-blind           | No working blue-sensitive cones.

Each chip shows its hex code. As the page says, "The atlas stores the
simulated colour, not a filter over the swatch — so these are values you can
copy." You can use these hex codes to check whether two colours in a design
stay distinct for colour-blind readers.

EVERY SPACE, AND THE METRICS
============================

A table of the colour's values:

  Row:           RGB
  What it shows: Red, green and blue, each 0 to 255.

  Row:           HSL
  What it shows: Hue in degrees, saturation and lightness as percentages.

  Row:           CIE L*a*b*
  What it shows: Lightness L (0 to 100) and the a (green to red) and b (blue
                 to yellow) axes.

  Row:           OKLab
  What it shows: The same idea in the newer OKLab space: L, a and b.

  Row:           OKLCH
  What it shows: OKLab as lightness L, chroma C and hue h in degrees.

  Row:           CMYK
  What it shows: Cyan, magenta, yellow and black, as percentages.

  Row:           Relative luminance
  What it shows: How much light the colour gives off, from 0 (black) to 1
                 (white). Contrast ratios are built from this.

  Row:           Chroma
  What it shows: Colourfulness, as OKLCH chroma.

  Row:           Vividness
  What it shows: A precomputed measure of how vivid the colour is.

  Row:           Perceptual uniformity
  What it shows: A precomputed score stored with the colour.

Values are shown to two, four or more decimal places. A dash means the value
is not in the record.

NEAREST IN THE ATLAS
====================

The last block shows the five atlas colours closest to this one, each with its
hex code and its distance, for example ΔE 2.10 — indistinguishable. The
distance words mean the same as in Find a colour's nearest neighbours
[find-nearest-colours.txt].

RESULT
======

You know whether the colour can carry body text on white or black, what it
becomes for red-, green- and blue-blind readers, its value in every notation,
and which catalogued colours are near it.
