==============================================================================
READ AN ARTICLE
==============================================================================
Find your way around a Learn article: header actions, facts, table of
contents, live demos, the quiz, printing and downloading.
Area: Website › Library › Learn   Updated: 2026-09-25

Every Learn article follows the same layout. This page walks through it from
top to bottom.

THE HEADER
==========

  - A small line reading Auric Artisan · Learn above the title.
  - The title and an introductory paragraph.
  - A row of facts, each with a bold label: the article's category and its
    number in the course (for example "Physics · 112"), how many live demos it
    has, the reading time (for example "~30 min"), and its subject.
  - Three actions:

  Action:       Download
  What it does: Opens a small menu: Markdown (".md — Formatted, portable
                text") or Plain text (".txt — Just the words"). The file is
                made in your browser from the article's text; interactive
                demos, figures and the quiz are left out.

  Action:       Print
  What it does: Opens your browser's print dialog.

  Action:       ← All Articles
  What it does: Returns to the Learn page.

Downloaded files are named after the article, without the date, for example
`the-color-of-heat.md`. Each starts with the title, the introduction and a
"Source:" line with the article's address. Press `Escape` to close the
Download menu.

THE TABLE OF CONTENTS
=====================

A Table of Contents lists the article's numbered sections. Select an entry to
jump to it. A reading-progress bar at the top of the window fills as you
scroll.

SECTIONS
========

Articles are divided into numbered sections, typically: an introduction,
several teaching sections each with a live demo, The terms, defined (a
glossary), What it means for color (practical takeaways), Test your
understanding, and Continue your journey. Sections fade in as you reach them.

LIVE DEMOS
==========

Each interactive lab appears in the article as a wide button labelled Live
demo, with the demo's name and Expand.

  1. Select the button. The demo opens in a panel over the page, marked Live,
     Article and Dockable.
  2. Use the demo's own controls — sliders, buttons, colour pickers — and read
     the explanation beside it.
  3. Use the panel buttons:

  Button:       Dock panel
  What it does: Docks the panel to the side so you can read the article beside
                the demo. Select again to undock.

  Button:       Full screen
  What it does: Enlarges the panel to fill the window. Select again to
                restore.

  Button:       Close
  What it does: Closes the panel. `Escape` does the same.

Docked and full screen are exclusive: turning one on turns the other off.

Demos run entirely in your browser and do not need an account.

TEST YOUR UNDERSTANDING
=======================

Near the end, Quick check asks multiple-choice questions one at a time — six
in recent articles. Choose an answer to see feedback, then select Next →. A
counter shows your place, for example Question 1 of 6.

CONTINUE YOUR JOURNEY
=====================

The last section suggests what to read next. On articles in the catalogue, the
page replaces the author's fixed suggestions with personalised ones, and a
line above them says, for example, Tailored to Physics · You've read 12 of 113
in the library. See Continue your journey [continue-your-journey.txt].

THE FOOT OF THE ARTICLE
=======================

A line gives the article's place in the course, for example Article 112 of
113.

KEEPING YOUR PLACE
==================

As you scroll, the article records how far you got, in this browser. Once you
pass 55% of the page, the article counts as read on the Learn page and in
Continue your journey. Reaching the bottom is not required.

RESULT
======

You can read, explore and test yourself on any article, print it or keep a
copy, and move on to a suggestion that fits what you have read.
