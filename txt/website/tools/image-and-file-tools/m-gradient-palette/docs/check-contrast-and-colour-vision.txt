==============================================================================
CHECK CONTRAST AND COLOUR VISION
==============================================================================
Check every step of a ramp as a background for a text colour with WCAG 2.2 and
APCA, and view the ramp as seen with three colour-vision deficiencies.
Area: Website › Tools › Image and file tools › M-Gradient Palette   Updated: 2026-09-25

CHECK TEXT ON EVERY STEP
========================

  1. Build your ramp on the Ramp tab.
  2. Open the Contrast tab.
  3. Under Text colour, select the swatch and choose the colour your text will
     be. The default is white, `#FFFFFF`.
  4. Read Your ramp, checked both ways. The note beside the heading names the
     text colour, for example against #FFFFFF.

Every step is treated as a background for text in the chosen colour, and
measured by two methods at once.

  Column:  Step
  Meaning: The step's swatch and hex value

  Column:  WCAG 2
  Meaning: The WCAG 2.2 contrast ratio, for example 7.90:1

  Column:  Rating
  Meaning: AAA, AA, AA large or fail

  Column:  APCA Lc
  Meaning: The APCA lightness contrast, signed

  Column:  Good for
  Meaning: What APCA considers the pair suitable for

  Column:  The old figure
  Meaning: The Lc an earlier version of this tool printed, struck through;
           highlighted where it differs by more than 20

WCAG 2.2
--------

The WCAG 2.2 contrast ratio compares the relative luminance of the two
colours, from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white).

  Rating   | Ratio         | Meaning
  ---------+---------------+--------------------------------
  AAA      | 7:1 or more   | Body text at the enhanced level
  AA       | 4.5:1 or more | Body text at the minimum level
  AA large | 3:1 or more   | Large text only
  fail     | Below 3:1     | Not enough for text

APCA
----

APCA, the Advanced Perceptual Contrast Algorithm (version APCA-W3 0.1.9 here),
measures lightness contrast in units called Lc. It treats dark text on a light
background and light text on a dark background differently, so it is signed:

  - a positive Lc means dark text on a lighter background;
  - a negative Lc means light text on a darker background.

With white text, a dark step gives a negative figure, for example −99.8 for
`#0B3D5C`.

  Absolute Lc | Good for
  ------------+---------------
  90 or more  | any text
  75 or more  | body text
  60 or more  | large text
  45 or more  | headlines only
  30 or more  | non-text only
  Below 30    | not for text

Black text on white gives Lc 106.04; white text on black gives −107.88.

Why two figures
---------------

WCAG 2.2 and APCA disagree by design: they measure different things on
different scales. A step can pass one and fail the other. Both are always
shown so you can see the disagreement rather than being handed whichever one
passes.

The samples
-----------

Under the table, three boxes show the sentence "The quick brown fox" in your
text colour on the first, middle and last steps, so you can judge by eye.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE TABLE SHOWS
=============================

At the top of the Contrast tab, The contrast figure passed text its own
algorithm fails compares, for five fixed pairs, what an earlier version of
this tool printed (It printed) with what APCA gives (APCA gives) and the
difference (Out by). The earlier version used the wrong scale and was missing
two parts of the algorithm, so it reported contrast about 1.4 times too high:
161.80 for black on white, where APCA gives 106.04. The footnote under your
own table says how far the old figure is off on your ramp, and how many steps
it would wrongly have passed at Lc 60.

WCAG 2.2 was already right confirms the WCAG figures: black on white gives
exactly 21.00, `#767676` on white gives 4.54, and `#595959` gives 7.00.

VIEW THE RAMP WITH A COLOUR-VISION DEFICIENCY
=============================================

On the Ramp tab, under Show it as seen by, choose:

  Button        | Simulates
  --------------+---------------------------------------------------------
  Normal vision | No simulation
  Protan        | Protanopia: no working long-wavelength (red) cones
  Deutan        | Deuteranopia: no working medium-wavelength (green) cones
  Tritan        | Tritanopia: no working short-wavelength (blue) cones

The simulation redraws the ramp, the dots on the hue circle, the three ramps
on the Spaces tab and the samples on the Contrast tab. It uses the model of
Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at full severity, applied in linear
light.

The figures, the contrast table, the copied values and the exports always use
the true colours.

What to look for
----------------

  - Two neighbouring steps that become hard to tell apart under a simulation
    are a problem for a data scale.
  - A ramp that runs from red to green can collapse to a single-hue ramp under
    Protan or Deutan; one that changes lightness steadily stays readable.

What the simulation does not cover
----------------------------------

It simulates complete loss of one cone type (dichromacy). It has no setting
for milder, partial forms (anomalous trichromacy), which are more common, so
the case the tool cannot show is the more likely one.
