==============================================================================
LIMITS AND ACCURACY
==============================================================================
What the ICC Profile Parser can and cannot read, how precise its figures are,
and what it is not.
Area: Website › Tools › Image and file tools › ICC Profile Parser   Updated: 2026-09-25

WHAT IT READS
=============

  - Standalone profile files of ICC version 2 and version 4, at the byte
    positions ICC.1:2010 (ISO 15076-1:2010) gives.
  - The whole header, the full tag table, and the tag types listed in the
    Reference [../docs/reference.txt]: text, descriptions, multi-language
    text, XYZ values, tone curves in every form, the `chad` matrix,
    signatures, and lut8 and lut16 tables.
  - A tag that cannot be decoded is reported as a finding, and the rest of the
    profile is still read.

WHAT IT CANNOT READ
===================

  Item:         Profiles embedded in images or PDFs
  What happens: Not read. Save the profile out as its own file first

  Item:         Version 4 `mAB `, `mBA ` tables
  What happens: Listed in the tag table but not decoded; no gamut hull is
                drawn from them

  Item:         `mpet` (multi-process elements)
  What happens: Its structure is read; the pipeline is not run, so its
                behaviour is not measured

  Item:         `ncl2` named colours
  What happens: Not resolved to colours and not listed on the page

  Item:         Private tags such as `vcgt`
  What happens: Listed with their type, not decoded

  Item:         Description and copyright text
  What happens: Read, but not printed on the page or in the reading

HOW THE FIGURES ARE FOUND, AND HOW PRECISE THEY ARE
===================================================

  Figure: Header fields
  Method: Read directly at the published offsets
  Notes:  Exact

  Figure: Profile ID match
  Method: MD5 recomputed with the flags, intent and ID fields zeroed
  Notes:  Exact

  Figure: Primaries
  Method: Colorants un-adapted with the profile's `chad` tag, or with Bradford
          from an assumed white
  Notes:  Exact with `chad`; an estimate without, and the caption says so

  Figure: Primaries Δu′v′
  Method: Largest per-primary distance in CIE 1976 u′v′, after adapting the
          reference into the PCS
  Notes:  Two adaptation methods tried; the closer is reported

  Figure: Curve fit
  Method: Peak difference from the reference transfer function over 257
          samples
  Notes:  Uses the red curve, or the grey curve for greyscale

  Figure: area, u′v′ and area, xy
  Method: Triangle area ratios
  Notes:  Area, not coverage

  Figure: covers of it
  Method: Polygon intersection in u′v′
  Notes:  At most 100%

  Figure: CIELAB hull
  Method: Every grid point of the A2B1 (or A2B0) table converted to CIELAB,
          then its outline
  Notes:  Up to 200,000 grid points

  Figure: Spectral locus
  Method: CIE 1931 2° observer, 380 to 700 nm at 5 nm
  Notes:  Taken from CIE 015:2018 data

CHOICES THE TOOL MAKES
======================

These are listed as Stand-in on the Data tab.

  - The six reference spaces. Identification means closest among sRGB, Rec.
    709, Display P3, Adobe RGB (1998), Rec. 2020 and ProPhoto RGB. Each
    definition is taken from its own publication; the choice of list is the
    tool's.
  - Severity. The grade of each rule (error, warning or note) is the tool's
    judgement. Each rule states its clause so you can apply your own.

WHAT IT IS NOT
==============

  - Not a device measurement. Everything on the page is a reading of a file.
    Whether the display, printer or press still behaves as the profile says is
    a question for a measuring instrument and the device. A profile that is
    internally perfect can describe a monitor that has drifted since
    calibration.
  - Not an official validator. The conformance check cites the specification
    clause by clause, but it is not the International Color Consortium's own
    validation tool.
  - Not a profile editor or converter. It does not change profiles or convert
    colours through them.
