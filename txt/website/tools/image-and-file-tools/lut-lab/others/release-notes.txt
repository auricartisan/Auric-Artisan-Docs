3 JUNE 2026 — THE MEASURED WORKBENCH
====================================

The earlier engine was replaced by the current workbench, with six tabs: Lab,
Method, Looks, Data, Export and Reference. The site's earlier user guide
carries a notice that it describes the engine replaced on this date.

What changed for you:

  - Exported .cube files use the standard sample order. Files written by the
    earlier version had their red and blue axes swapped, so they looked right
    when read back by the same tool and wrong everywhere else. Re-export any
    `.cube` you made with the earlier version.
  - The overlay shows what the transform crushed. The earlier out-of-gamut
    overlay flagged pure colours, black and white even when nothing had
    changed them. The new overlay marks only pixels the transform drove to
    white or black, in two colours.
  - "Cine Log to Rec.709" is now "Log-ish lift". The arithmetic is unchanged;
    the old name claimed a log encoding and a conversion that were never
    applied.
  - The Reinhard tone-map runs in linear light, with an exposure control. Mid
    grey now comes out where the operator puts it.
  - Two features were withdrawn: the ACEScct to ACEScg look, which turned 114
    of 256 input codes pure white, and a 32-bit float export that could not
    produce anything but 8-bit values.
  - New readouts: CIEDE2000 mean, 95th percentile and largest difference;
    clipped share; and a hue circle in CIELAB with hue, lightness and chroma
    shifts.
  - Your own picture can be the test scene.
  - The export header states the sample order and the 8-bit precision, and the
    Export tab says whether a size's grid lands on 8-bit values.
  - New exports: the hue sweep as CSV and a full report as JSON, and a link
    that restores your settings.

25 MAY 2026 — EARLIER VERSION
=============================

The LUT Lab user guide was published in the site's documentation library. It
describes the earlier engine and is now marked as superseded.
