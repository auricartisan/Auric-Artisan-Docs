==============================================================================
GETTING STARTED WITH THE VISION LAB
==============================================================================
Open the Vision Lab, learn the five views, and run your first simulation.
Area: Website › Tools › Accessibility and vision   Updated: 2026-09-25

OPEN IT
=======

Go to https://auricartisan.com/tool/vision-simulation/. No account is needed
to use it.

THE SCREEN, TOP TO BOTTOM
=========================

Masthead
--------

The kicker "Auric Artisan · Vision", the headline "Seventy-eight ways of
seeing, simulated.", and a line explaining that every condition runs the same
ten reference swatches through the published models, in linear light, so two
cards differ because the conditions differ. A rail of facts follows:
Conditions 78, Categories 11, Brettel 1997 · Viénot 1999 · Machado 2009, and
Read the API reference →.

API hint
--------

"Automate this — 5 API credits per call to `POST /v1/vision/audit`." It tells
developers that the palette audit is also available from the REST API, and
that API credits are a separate balance from tool tokens. Select API docs to
read about the endpoint, or × to dismiss the hint on every tool page.

The view bar
------------

Five buttons switch between the lab's views. The lab remembers which one you
last used.

  View:           Workbench
  What it is for: One condition taken apart: a specimen, a severity dial, the
                  model, and measurements

  View:           Catalogue
  What it is for: Every condition as a card; a card opens a detailed floating
                  panel

  View:           Compare
  What it is for: One source shown under many conditions at once, as a contact
                  sheet

  View:           Palette audit
  What it is for: Every pair in your palette checked under all 17 colour
                  conditions

  View:           Analytics
  What it is for: The whole collection mapped by category and severity

The notice
----------

At the foot of the lab: "Educational visualisation only. Not a medical
simulation, a diagnostic tool, or a substitute for professional eye care — and
screen colours are not calibrated."

TWO KINDS OF CONDITION
======================

  - Per-colour transforms (17). Colour-vision deficiencies, cone isolation and
    colour tints. Every colour has an exact answer: the lab computes what each
    colour becomes.
  - Field effects (61). Cataract, glaucoma, retinal and optic-nerve conditions
    and others. They change where and how clearly you see (blur, dark or lost
    regions, glare, distortion). On a card, the reference swatches are left as
    they are and the loss is painted over them, "because colour survives
    glaucoma and the field does not."

YOUR FIRST SIMULATION
=====================

  1. On the Workbench, look at the stage in the middle. With the Interface
     specimen and Split stage, it shows a small interface mock-up as it
     normally looks and as it looks with deuteranopia.
  2. Select the Base colour field on the left and type your brand colour, for
     example `#D3AF37`. Next to it, "under condition →" shows the colour it
     becomes.
  3. In the list on the right, select Protanopia. The stage, the base colour
     and the measurements update.
  4. Read What collapses under the stage: reference colour pairs that were
     clearly different and now are not.
  5. Read Measured · base colour: how far your colour moved (ΔE76 and ΔE2000),
     how many of the 45 reference pairs were lost, and the contrast of the
     mock-up's button label on its fill.
  6. Drag the Severity slider down to see a milder form of the deficiency.

The result: you know what your colour becomes for someone with protanopia, and
which colours they can no longer tell apart.
