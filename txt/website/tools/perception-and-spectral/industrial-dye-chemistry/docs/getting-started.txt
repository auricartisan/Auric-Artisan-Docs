OPEN THE TOOL
=============

Go to
https://auricartisan.com/tool/general/spectral-tools/industrial-dye-chemistry/.
The page opens on the Lab tab with a default bath: one Weak-acid indicator
(standing for the methyl-orange family) at 34.0 µM, pKa 3.5, at pH 3.50, in a
1.0 cm path, lit by D65 daylight.

A note strip above the tabs, which cannot be closed, says what an earlier
version of the page got wrong and links to the Method and Safety tabs.

A TOUR OF THE LAB TAB
=====================

The rail (left), top to bottom:

  - The bath — pH, Path length and Run the titration.
  - The light — the illuminant menu.
  - The components — the list of dyes in the bath, a menu of dyes to add, and
    Add, Mute and Remove.
  - The selected dye — its name, what it stands for, and its pKa and
    Concentration sliders.
  - Reset the bath.

The stage (right):

  - The two forms, and what you measure — the spectrum plot for the selected
    dye, 380–780 nm, with a legend: Acid form `HA` (dashed), Base form `A⁻`
    (dashed), What you measure (the filled curve, their weighted sum) and
    Isosbestic (a green circle and label where the two forms cross).
  - Three rows of readouts: sRGB, CIE L*, a*, b*, Base fraction; Peak
    absorbance, λmax, Beer–Lambert holds, Isosbestic point; Dominant λ,
    Excitation purity, Inside sRGB, Observer.
  - The same bath under every light this tool holds — one swatch per
    illuminant, with the colour difference from the current light, and a card
    for F11 marked not held.

READ THE DEFAULT BATH
=====================

  1. Look at the plot. The acid form peaks near 508 nm and the base form near
     462 nm. At pH 3.50, equal to the dye's pKa, half the dye is in each form,
     so Base fraction reads 0.500 and the filled curve sits halfway between
     them.
  2. Find the green circle: Isosbestic point reads about 480 nm, where the two
     forms absorb equally.
  3. Read the colour: sRGB `#FFBF9F`, a pale orange-pink, with CIE L* 84.8 and
     a*, b* 28.2, 28.9.
  4. Read Peak absorbance 0.630 at λmax 485 nm, and Beer–Lambert holds yes, A
     0.63: the bath is inside the range where absorbance grows in proportion
     to concentration.
  5. Look at the lights row: the same bath differs by about 1.60 ΔE₀₀ under
     D50, 6.57 under A and 0.74 under E.
  6. Select Run the titration. The pH climbs from 0 to 14 in about six seconds
     and the swatch moves from pink (`#FF96C7` at pH 0) to yellow (`#FFF17A`
     at pH 14).

You should now see a bath whose colour, spectrum and readouts respond to every
control.
