==============================================================================
COLOR INSPECTOR
==============================================================================
Use the Color Inspector to read one colour in every format, check it for
contrast and colour-vision safety, find its name, build scales and harmonies,
and export it as code.
Area: Website › Tools › Colour workspace   Updated: 2026-09-25

The Color Inspector takes one colour and shows you everything the site can
work out about it. A plain-language summary tells you how light, saturated,
warm and readable the colour is. Around it sit ten tabs of detail: more than
forty notations, contrast against real surfaces, how the colour looks with
colour-vision deficiencies, harmonies, the nearest named colours and industry
swatches, colour psychology, tint and shade scales, spectral science, and
ready-to-paste code.

Use it when you meet a colour and want to know whether you can use it, and
how. It is also the tool behind the site's `?color=` links: add `?color=` and
a hex code to any page address and the Inspector opens over that page.

The Inspector is one of the six Essentials in the Colour Tools. It opens in a
floating panel; for moving, resizing and closing panels, see Open the Colour
Tools and manage their panels [launcher-and-panels.txt].

HOW TO OPEN IT
==============

  - Launcher: right-click the page, choose Colour tools › All colour tools…
    (or press `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `C`), then select Color Inspector in the
    Essentials group.
  - Right-click a colour: when you right-click a hex code or a colour swatch
    on the site, choose Inspect in Colour Tools. The Inspector opens with that
    colour.
  - A link: add `?color=` and a colour to any page address, for example
    `https://auricartisan.com/?color=CEA1B5`. Add `&ctab=contrast` to open on
    a tab, or `&on=FFFFFF` to add a comparison colour. The full list of
    accepted forms is in Open the Colour Tools and manage their panels
    [launcher-and-panels.txt].

Opened without a colour, the Inspector starts on the site's gold, `#D3AF37`.

SCREEN TOUR
===========

The panel has three areas: a strip of ten tabs across the top, a sidebar, and
the main area that shows the selected tab. In a narrow panel the sidebar moves
above the main area.

The sidebar
-----------

  - The hero swatch. A large swatch of the colour with its hex code, its
    closest colour name, and four badges: the hue family, the correlated
    colour temperature in kelvin, the CIE L* lightness, and a readability
    badge (AA on white, AA on black or Decorative). Select the swatch to copy
    the hex code. When the name is only approximate, it starts with `≈`. If
    the colour came from a link with transparency, the swatch shows a
    checkerboard and an `α` tag; if the link held a fragment the site had to
    interpret, a tag shows the text it started from.
  - The colour field. The colour swatch opens your browser's colour picker,
    the text box takes a hex code, and two icon buttons follow: the dice
    (Random color tooltip, "Jump to a perceptually balanced random color")
    picks a random colour of medium saturation and lightness, and the drop
    (Screen picker tooltip, "Sample any pixel on screen") lets you pick a
    colour from anywhere on your screen. The drop only appears in browsers
    that support picking colours from the screen.
  - Read-out. Nine cells: RGB, HSL, OKLCh, CIE L*, Luminance, Chroma, Temp, On
    white and On black. Select any cell to copy its value.
  - Compare. Chips for comparison colours. They come from a link (every colour
    after the first, and any `on=` colours), and every colour you move away
    from in this panel is added too, most recent first, up to eight. Select a
    chip to inspect that colour instead. The section is hidden while it is
    empty.
  - Actions. Copy hex, Copy CSS, Share link, Save and Download swatch. See
    Outputs and exports.
  - Open in. Eight buttons that open another Colour Tool with this colour:
    Contrast System, Harmony Studio, Space Converter, Pantone & Named,
    Accessibility Lab, Formulation, CMYK Soft-Proof and Psychology.

Almost every swatch in the tabs is a button. Select one and the Inspector
switches to that colour, and the colour you were on goes into Compare, so you
can always step back.

The tab strip
-------------

The tabs are Overview, Formats, Contrast, Vision, Harmony, Names, Psychology,
Scales, Science and Export. When the panel is too narrow to show them all,
arrow buttons appear at each end of the strip to scroll it.

With a tab focused, `←` and `→` move to the previous or next tab (wrapping
round), and `Home` and `End` jump to the first and last.

Overview
--------

  - Summary. A sentence or two in plain words, for example: "#D3AF37 — closest
    named color … is a light yellow, vivid. It is warm (3352 K) and carries
    body text on black but not on white." It describes lightness (from
    near-black to near-white), saturation (from achromatic to "at the edge of
    what sRGB can show"), temperature, which text use the colour can carry,
    and the colour's dominant wavelength.
  - Live preview. The colour as a background with a headline, 14 px body copy,
    11.5 px small print and a Primary action button, all in whichever of black
    or white reads better on it. Below it: White text and Black text ratios,
    and Best ink.
  - Channels. Bars for R, G, B and alpha, and for hue, saturation, lightness
    and OKLCh chroma.
  - Quick numbers. Hex, RGB, HSL, HSV, CMYK, CIELAB, CIELCh, OKLCh, luminance
    Y, hue family, temperature and total ink.
  - Shades → tints. Eleven steps from dark to light with your colour in the
    middle, mixed in OKLCh. Select a step to inspect it.

Formats
-------

A button, Copy the full read-out (JSON), and 43 notations in five groups.
Select any row to copy just that value.

  Group:     Web & CSS (19)
  Notations: HEX, HEX (short), RGB, RGBA, RGB %, HSL, HSLA, HSV, CMYK, CIE XYZ
             (D65), CIELAB (D65), CIELCh (D65)

  Group:     -
  Notations: OKLab, OKLCh, Linear sRGB, HEX + alpha, CSS modern rgb, HWB, HSI

  Group:     Wide gamut (5)
  Notations: Display P3, Adobe RGB, Rec. 2020 (as CSS `color()` values),
             ACEScg (linear), Linear sRGB

  Group:     Perceptual & CIE (9)
  Notations: CIE xyY, CIE u′v′, CIELAB (D50), CIELCh (D50), CIELUV, LCh(uv),
             Hunter Lab, IPT, JzAzBz

  Group:     Video & broadcast (3)
  Notations: YCbCr, YUV, YIQ

  Group:     Code (7)
  Notations: Decimal, Android (ARGB), Swift UIColor, SwiftUI, Flutter, OpenGL
             vec4, .NET ARGB

Contrast
--------

Against real surfaces is a table that treats your colour as text on seven
standard surfaces: White `#FFFFFF`, Paper `#F5F5F3`, Light gray `#E5E5E5`, Mid
gray `#767676`, Charcoal `#333333`, Near black `#111111` and Black `#000000`.
When the Inspector was opened by a link, two more rows show This page's
background and This page's text, sampled from the page underneath. Every
comparison colour adds a Compared color row.

  Column:  Ratio
  Meaning: The WCAG 2.x contrast ratio.

  Column:  Body / Large
  Meaning: The WCAG level for normal and for large text: AAA, AA or Fail.

  Column:  UI
  Meaning: Pass at 3:1 or more, the WCAG threshold for icons, borders and
           other interface parts.

  Column:  APCA Lc
  Meaning: The APCA lightness contrast of your colour as text on that surface.

  Column:  APCA verdict
  Meaning: AAA (Lc 90 or more), AA (75), Body (60) or Fail.

  Column:  ISO mod.
  Meaning: The ISO 9241-3 luminance modulation and its rating.

  Column:  Preview
  Meaning: "Aa" in your colour on the surface.

A line under the table gives the reverse reading: white and paper text on your
colour, in Lc.

Nearest accessible version offers four swatches, each your colour with its
lightness changed and its hue kept, just enough to reach AA body text on white
(4.5:1), AAA body text on white (7:1), AA body text on black and AAA body text
on black. Each shows the new hex and ratio, or "already passes". Select one to
inspect it.

As a background shows your colour behind white text and behind black text,
with the ratio and the body and large-text levels for each.

Vision
------

  - How this color is seen. A table of typical vision and four colour-vision
    deficiencies: Protanopia (no long-wave, red-sensitive cones), Deuteranopia
    (no medium-wave, green-sensitive cones), Tritanopia (no short-wave,
    blue-sensitive cones) and Achromatopsia (no colour discrimination). For
    each: the simulated colour, its hex (select it to inspect), and the ΔE2000
    shift from the original. A shift above ΔE 10 means the colour reads as a
    clearly different hue.
  - Filtered preview. Five sample cards of your colour, one per vision type,
    drawn through live filters.
  - Grayscale survival. Your colour next to its grey equivalent, with the
    grey's hex and L*. This is how it fares in black-and-white print,
    photocopies and e-ink.
  - Confusion risk. Shown only when there are comparison colours. For each
    one: the ΔE between it and your colour for typical vision, the vision type
    under which they are closest, the ΔE there, and a verdict: Distinct (ΔE 10
    or more), Tight (5 to 10) or Confusable (under 5).

Harmony
-------

Every scheme is rotated in OKLCh, so companion colours keep the lightness and
chroma of your colour instead of drifting the way HSL rotations do. The tab
shows eight schemes, each swatch labelled with its hue and OKLCh lightness:

  Scheme              | Hue offsets
  --------------------+----------------------------------------------
  Complementary       | 0°, +180°
  Split-Complementary | 0°, +150°, +210°
  Analogous           | −30°, 0°, +30°
  Triadic             | 0°, +120°, +240°
  Tetradic            | 0°, +90°, +180°, +270°
  Square              | 0°, +90°, +180°, +270°
  Monochromatic       | Six steps of lightness at your hue and chroma
  Double Split        | 0°, +30°, −30°, +150°, +210°

Tetradic and Square use the same 90° spacing in this tab. Accessible triad
then shows the triadic colours adjusted to reach 4.5:1 on white and,
separately, on black, with each ratio.

Names
-----

  - Closest named color. Your colour beside the nearest of 1,058 named
    colours, the ΔE2000 between them, and what that distance means, from
    "indistinguishable" (under ΔE 1) to "the nearest entry, but visibly
    different" (ΔE 10 or more).
  - Industry systems. The three closest entries in each of five systems:
    HTML/CSS (141 colours), Pantone Solid C (105), RAL Classic (148), NCS (56)
    and Crayola (111).
  - Similar named colors. The next 17 names by ΔE2000.

This tab downloads its data the first time you open it, and shows "Loading
names data…" until it arrives.

Psychology
----------

  - Emotional read. Emotion words for the colour's hue family at its
    lightness, and meters for Warmth (Warm, Neutral or Cool), Energy (High,
    Moderate or Calm), Saturation pull (OKLCh chroma) and Lightness (L*). Very
    light colours read softer and calmer; very dark ones read more serious and
    grounded.
  - Personality. Trait words.
  - Brand fit. A sentence on where this hue family suits a brand.
  - Cultural meaning. Positive and negative associations by region or
    tradition, for example China, India and Japan for red.

This tab also downloads its data the first time you open it.

Scales
------

  - Tints, Shades and Tones. Ten steps each from your colour towards white,
    black and mid grey, mixed in OKLCh. Tones lower the chroma and keep the
    hue.
  - Design-token ramp. Eleven steps named 50 to 950, each at a fixed OKLCh
    lightness, with chroma eased off towards the ends and limited to what sRGB
    can show at that lightness. Each step shows its hex and its contrast ratio
    on white and on black, and the step nearest your colour is marked with
    `◂`.
  - CSS custom properties. The ramp as `--color-50` to `--color-950` inside
    `:root`, with a Copy button.

Science
-------

  - Where this color sits in the spectrum. Dominant wavelength (with its band:
    violet, blue, cyan, green, yellow, orange or red), excitation purity, CIE
    x, y, CIE u′, v′, tristimulus Y, correlated colour temperature and a
    temperature description from Very warm to Very cool. Purples and magentas
    have no single wavelength, so they show a complementary wavelength and
    "Line of purples" instead.
  - Approximate reflectance. A spectrum strip from 380 to 780 nm, masked to
    show roughly which light a surface of this colour would send back.
  - Gamut headroom. How much of the available chroma your colour uses at its
    lightness and hue in sRGB, the current and maximum chroma, and a 12-step
    ramp from grey to the most saturated version.
  - Coordinates in other gamuts. The colour's R, G, B values in sRGB, Display
    P3, Adobe RGB and Rec. 2020.
  - On press. A simple CMYK split, the total ink, and a pass mark for total
    ink up to 300% (a warning up to 320%). Most web presses cap total ink
    around 300%.

Export
------

  - Share this read-out. A read-only box with the current page address plus
    `?color=`, and three buttons: Copy link, Swatch file and Save to library.
    A note reminds you that `?on=ffffff` adds a comparison colour and
    `&ctab=contrast` opens a tab.
  - Six code blocks, each with a Copy button: CSS custom properties, SCSS,
    Tailwind, Android colors.xml, SwiftUI and JSON — everything above.

WALKTHROUGHS
============

Check whether a brand colour can carry text
-------------------------------------------

  1. Open the Inspector with your colour, for example with `?color=D3AF37` on
     any page.
  2. Read the summary on Overview. For `#D3AF37` it says the colour "carries
     body text on black but not on white".
  3. Open the Contrast tab. The White row shows a ratio of 2.11 and Fail for
     body text; the Black row shows 9.96 and AAA.
  4. Under Nearest accessible version, select AA body text on white.

The Inspector switches to a darker version of your gold that reaches 4.5:1 on
white, and your original colour moves into Compare so you can go back to it.

Check that two colours stay distinct for colour-blind readers
-------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Open the Inspector with both colours in one link, for example
     `?color=D3AF37,4F9D69`.
  2. Open the Vision tab.
  3. Read Confusion risk. It names the vision type under which the two colours
     are closest and gives a verdict.

If the verdict is Confusable or Tight, do not rely on the colour difference
alone: add a label, an icon or a pattern.

Export a colour as design tokens
--------------------------------

  1. Set the colour you want in the colour field.
  2. Open the Export tab.
  3. Select Copy above the block you need, for example Tailwind.

The clipboard now holds a `tailwind.config.js` snippet with a `brand` scale
from 50 to 950. The CSS custom properties block for `#D3AF37` starts like
this:

    :root {
      --brand: #d3af37;
      --brand-rgb: 211 175 55;
      --brand-oklch: oklch(0.7655 0.1385 91.55);
      --brand-ink: #000000;
      --brand-50: #fff5c2;
      --brand-100: #ffe998;
      /* … through --brand-950 */
    }

Share exactly what you are looking at
-------------------------------------

  1. Open the tab you want the other person to see, for example Names.
  2. In the sidebar, select Share link.

A message confirms "Share link copied" with the address. The link opens the
current page with the Inspector on the same colour and the same tab.

ACCURACY AND LIMITS
===================

  - Terms used on this page. WCAG contrast ratio: the ratio between the
    relative luminance of two colours, from 1:1 to 21:1. WCAG 2 asks for 4.5:1
    for normal text (AA), 3:1 for large text and interface parts, and 7:1 for
    AAA. APCA Lc: a newer measure of perceived lightness contrast, roughly
    −108 to +106. Positive values mean dark text on a lighter background,
    negative values light text on a darker one. ΔE2000: the CIEDE2000 colour
    difference; under about 1 two colours look the same side by side. OKLCh: a
    colour model with lightness, chroma and hue that changes evenly to the
    eye. Colour-vision deficiency (CVD): reduced or missing sensitivity in one
    type of cone.
  - APCA verdicts are a guide. WCAG 3, which may adopt APCA, is still a draft.
    The AAA, AA and Body labels are the site's reading of the published APCA
    guidance, not a conformance result. For a WCAG 2.2 audit, use the Ratio
    column.
  - ISO 9241-3 ratings. The modulation is (Lmax − Lmin) / (Lmax + Lmin) from 0
    to 1. The tool rates 0.7 and over Excellent, 0.5 Good, 0.3 Marginal and
    below that Poor.
  - Vision simulations are approximations. They model full-strength dichromacy
    with simple fixed matrices. Real colour vision varies from person to
    person, and milder forms (anomalous trichromacy) are more common than the
    full forms shown. Use the results to find risks, not to certify a design.
  - Temperature. Correlated colour temperature is calculated with McCamy's
    formula. It is meaningful for near-neutral colours and only indicative for
    saturated ones.
  - Reflectance is a heuristic. The curve explains the colour; it is not a
    spectrophotometer reading and cannot be used to match pigments.
  - CMYK is arithmetic. The On press split is a simple conversion, not an ICC
    profile conversion. Soft-proof in CMYK Soft-Proof [cmyk-soft-proof.txt]
    before sending artwork to print.
  - Named-system matches are approximate. Pantone, RAL and NCS entries are
    published sRGB approximations of printed swatches, so the tool always
    shows the ΔE distance rather than calling a match exact.
  - sRGB only. Colours are handled as sRGB. Transparency from a link is shown
    and exported, but contrast is calculated on the solid colour.
  - Page sampling. The "This page's" rows use the page's main background and
    text colour, not the colour behind a particular element.
  - Network. The Names and Psychology tabs download their data from the site
    the first time you open them. If that fails, the tab says "That data set
    could not be loaded."
