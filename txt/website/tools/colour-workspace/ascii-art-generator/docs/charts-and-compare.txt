==============================================================================
CHARTS, BENCHMARK AND COMPARE
==============================================================================
The measurements, charts and benchmark in the Analysis tab, and the character
set comparison in the Compare tab.
Area: Website › Tools › Colour workspace   Updated: 2026-09-25

The Analysis and Compare tabs explain why the art looks the way it does. You
do not need them to make art; they help when a result bands, looks flat or
converts slowly.

THE ANALYSIS TAB
================

This render
-----------

The left column repeats the facts of the last conversion:

  - Grid, Characters and Render (time in milliseconds);
  - Character set, and Ramp (the number of glyphs, for example 10 glyphs);
  - Sampler and Dithering.

Benchmark
---------

Run benchmark converts the current source ten times with the current settings
and reports the average, for example Avg: 18.4 ms (10 runs), and the Min, Max
and Spread. Use it to see which setting makes conversion slow. With the Image
source and no picture loaded, it reads Load an image first.

The charts
----------

Six charts. Each has a ⛶ button, and you can also double-click a chart, to
open it full screen. Close the full-screen chart with ×, `Esc`, or by
selecting the dark background.

  Chart:            Glyph density calibration
  What it shows:    The ink of each character in the current set, measured by
                    drawing it at 32 × 32 pixels, sorted light to dark.
  What to look for: A smooth rise. Big jumps or flat runs mean some tones have
                    no matching character.

  Chart:            Character frequency
  What it shows:    How often each character appears in the latest output.
  What to look for: A spike at one end means gamma or contrast is crushing the
                    mid-tones.

  Chart:            Top character distribution
  What it shows:    The twelve most frequent characters as a ring, with the
                    rest grouped as one slice.
  What to look for: Whether a few characters dominate the art.

  Chart:            Sobel edge field
  What it shows:    The strength of edges in the source picture; bright means
                    a strong edge.
  What to look for: Where edge-aware glyph selection can act. Where it is
                    dark, Edge weight has nothing to work on.

  Chart:            Sampling comparison
  What it shows:    The same source through area averaging, bilinear and point
                    sampling.
  What to look for: Point sampling breaking fine detail into noise.

  Chart:            Render timeline
  What it shows:    Conversion time for the last hundred conversions.
  What to look for: Where live updates stop keeping up with the sliders.

The charts redraw after every conversion and when you open the tab.

THE COMPARE TAB
===============

Measure all sets draws every character of all seven sets at 32 × 32 pixels,
measures how much ink each one has, and fills a table:

  Column:  Charset
  Meaning: The set: standard, detailed, blocks, minimal, symbols, lines or
           braille.

  Column:  Glyphs
  Meaning: The number of characters, including the space.

  Column:  Min density
  Meaning: The ink of the lightest character, from 0 to 1.

  Column:  Max density
  Meaning: The ink of the darkest character.

  Column:  Range
  Meaning: Max minus min. A wider range carries more contrast.

  Column:  Monotonic
  Meaning: ✓ if every character has at least as much ink as the one before it,
           in the set's own order; ✗ if not.

A set that is not monotonic has at least one character darker than the one
after it, and a smooth gradient drawn with it shows bands. More glyphs carry
more detail; a wider range carries more contrast. They are not the same thing.

The measurement uses the fonts on your device, so results can differ slightly
between computers. Your custom ramp is not in the table; when it is in use,
its densities show in the Glyph density calibration chart.
