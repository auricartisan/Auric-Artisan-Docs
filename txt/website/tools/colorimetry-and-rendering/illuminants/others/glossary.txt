==============================================================================
ILLUMINANTS GLOSSARY
==============================================================================
Plain-language definitions of the colour-science terms used in the Standard
Illuminants lab.
Area: Website › Tools › Colorimetry and rendering   Updated: 2026-09-25

Blackbody (Planckian radiator) — an idealised object whose emitted light
depends only on its temperature, described by Planck's law. Tungsten lamps are
close to one.

Chromatic adaptation — the eye's adjustment to the colour of the prevailing
light, so that a white sheet still looks white under warm or cool light.

Chromatic adaptation transform (CAT) — a calculation that predicts chromatic
adaptation. It maps XYZ into a cone-like space, scales each channel by the
ratio of two white points, and maps back. Bradford, CAT02, CAT16, Von Kries
(HPE) and Sharp differ in the cone-like matrix.

Chromaticity — the colour of a light or surface without its brightness,
written as two coordinates.

Chromaticity diagram — a plot of chromaticity. The lab offers CIE 1931 xy, CIE
1960 uv (where Duv is defined) and CIE 1976 u′v′ (more evenly spaced).

CIE — the International Commission on Illumination, which publishes the
standard observers, illuminants and formulas used here.

Colour matching functions (CMFs) — three curves describing how a standard
observer matches colours. Weighting a spectrum by them gives X, Y and Z.

Colour rendering index (CRI) — a score of how faithfully a light renders test
colours compared with a reference light at the same CCT. Ra is the mean of the
first eight scores (R1–R8); R9 is saturated red.

Complementary wavelength — for a colour that has no dominant wavelength (a
purple), the wavelength opposite it through the white point. The lab shows it
with a minus sign.

Correlated colour temperature (CCT) — the temperature, in kelvin, of the
blackbody whose colour is closest to the light. Only meaningful when Duv is
small.

Daylight locus — the curve of CIE daylight chromaticities, slightly above the
Planckian locus.

D-series illuminants — CIE daylight illuminants D50, D55, D65 and D75, built
from three daylight basis functions (S0, S1, S2).

Dominant wavelength — the single wavelength that, mixed with white, matches
the colour's hue.

Duv — the signed distance of a chromaticity from the Planckian locus in the
CIE 1960 uv plane. Positive is greenish, negative is pinkish.

Efficacy (luminous efficacy of radiation) — lumens per watt of radiant power:
how much visible light a spectrum delivers for its power.

Equal-energy illuminant (E) — a theoretical light with the same power at every
wavelength. Its chromaticity is x = y = 1/3.

Expanded uncertainty (U) — the standard uncertainty multiplied by a coverage
factor k. With k = 2 it covers about 95% of cases for a normal distribution.

Illuminant — a light source defined by a table of relative spectral power.

Isotemperature line — a short line across the Planckian locus joining
chromaticities that share the same CCT.

Metamerism — two surfaces that match under one light but not under another.
The metamerism index in the lab is the average CIEDE2000 difference of 14 test
samples between two lights.

Monte Carlo method — estimating uncertainty by repeating a calculation many
times with randomly perturbed inputs and measuring the spread.

Observer (standard observer) — the CIE's average human colour vision for a 2°
(1931) or 10° (1964) field. The lab holds only the 2° observer.

PCHIP — piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation; a resampling method that does
not overshoot near narrow peaks.

Planckian locus — the curve of blackbody chromaticities as temperature
changes.

Purity (excitation purity) — how far a colour lies from white towards its
dominant wavelength, as a percentage.

Spectral locus — the horseshoe-shaped edge of the chromaticity diagram, made
by single wavelengths.

Spectral power distribution (SPD) — the relative power a light emits at each
wavelength.

Sprague interpolation — a smooth resampling method recommended by CIE 167:2005
for evenly spaced spectral data.

Stand-in — a dataset built to illustrate a shape, not the published table.
Readings from a stand-in are never reported as standard values.

Test colour samples (TCS) — the 14 reflectance samples CIE 13.3 uses to
compute CRI. The lab approximates them.

TM-30 — the IES method for colour rendition using 99 samples and two indices,
Rf (fidelity) and Rg (gamut). Not computed by the lab.

Tristimulus values (X, Y, Z) — the three numbers that describe a colour for
the standard observer. Y is related to brightness.

White point — the chromaticity of an illuminant, used as the reference white
for colour calculations.
